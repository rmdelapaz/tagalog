Mga Batayan ng Gramatika - Grammar Essentials

The small words and patterns that make everything click into place

You've met sounds, sentences, verbs, and hundreds of words. This lesson is the connective tissue — the handful of high-frequency patterns that separate "understandable" from "natural": how to say you have something, how to glue words together, the full pronoun family, the tiny particles Filipinos sprinkle everywhere, pointing words, and comparisons.

1. May, Mayroon & Wala — Having & Existing

English uses "have" and "there is/are." Tagalog has a neat little system for both, and its opposite, wala (none / there isn't).

The three words

  • May — comes right before the thing, no marker needed. May pera ako. (I have money.) · May tao sa labas. (There's someone outside.)
  • Mayroon (colloquial meron) — the fuller form; use it with a pronoun (taking the linker) or when answering on its own. Mayroon akong pera. (I have money.) · Mayroon ba? (Is there any?)
  • Wala — the negative of both. Wala akong pera. (I have no money.) · Walang tao. (There's no one.)

See the pattern

  • May kotse siya. → Wala siyang kotse. (He has a car → He has no car.)
  • Mayroon kang oras? → Wala akong oras. (Do you have time? → I have no time.)
  • May klase ba ngayon? → Wala, walang klase. (Is there class today? → No, there's no class.)
Quick tip: Wala and mayroon take the linker -ng before a pronoun — walang tao, mayroong pagkain — which is exactly what the next section is about.

2. The Linker: na / -ng — Tagalog's Superglue

When one word describes another (adjective + noun, number + noun, "want" + verb), Tagalog joins them with a linker. It's tiny but everywhere, and choosing the right form is purely mechanical:

If the first word ends in…Use…Example
a voweladd -ngmaganda → magandang babae (beautiful woman)
the letter nchange n to -ngmalinis? → isang mansanas (one apple)
any other consonantadd na (separate word)mabait → mabait na tao (kind person)

Same idea, both orders

The linker works whether the adjective comes first or second — a very common flexibility:

  • malaking bahay = bahay na malaki (big house)
  • bagong sapatos = sapatos na bago (new shoes)
  • Gusto kong kumain. (I want to eat.) — linker joins "want" to the verb
Why it matters: forget the linker and you'll say "maganda babae," which sounds as off as "beautiful the woman." Get the linker right and even simple sentences suddenly sound fluent.

3. The Full Pronoun Family (ang / ng / sa)

You already know the ang-pronouns (ako, ikaw, siya…). But Tagalog pronouns come in three matching sets, one for each of the markers you learned in Lesson 2. Learn them as rows and possession and objects fall into place.

MeaningANG (subject)NG (of / by — possessive)SA (to / for)
I / me / myakoko(sa) akin
you (sing.)ikaw / kamo(sa) iyo
he / shesiyaniya(sa) kaniya
we (incl.)tayonatin(sa) atin
we (excl.)kaminamin(sa) amin
you (plural)kayoninyo(sa) inyo
theysilanila(sa) kanila

The three sets in action

  • Ang bahay ko. (My house.) — NG-form shows possession
  • Mahal niya ako. (He/She loves me.) — NG doer + ANG receiver
  • Ibigay mo sa akin. (Give it to me.) — NG doer + SA recipient
  • Para sa iyo ito. (This is for you.)
Memory hook: the NG-forms (ko, mo, niya) mean "my, your, his/her" when they follow a noun, and "by me/you/him" when they follow a verb. Same little words, two everyday jobs.

4. Enclitic Particles — Tagalog's Secret Seasoning

These tiny words carry huge amounts of meaning. They slot in right after the first word of the sentence and change tone, timing, and politeness. Master a handful and you'll sound Filipino, not textbook.

ParticleMeaningExample
naalready / nowKumain na ako. (I already ate.)
pastill / yet / moreKumakain pa ako. (I'm still eating.)
baturns it into a questionKumain ka ba? (Did you eat?)
po / oporespectSalamat po. (Thank you, respectfully.)
lang (lamang)only / justIsa lang. (Just one.)
din / rinalso / tooAko rin. (Me too.)
daw / rawthey say / reportedlyDarating daw siya. (He's coming, they say.)
ngaindeed / please (softener)Pakiabot nga. (Please pass it.)
palaoh! (sudden realization)Ikaw pala! (Oh, it's you!)
din vs rin, daw vs raw: use rin/raw after a vowel and din/daw after a consonant — ako rin but siya din… wait: siya rin (vowel), gusto ko din (consonant). It's the same ear-rule as the linker.

5. Demonstratives — This, That & That-Over-There

Like the place words dito / diyan / doon from Lesson 7, Tagalog "this/that" comes in three distances:

this - near me] --> D[The speaker] B[IYAN
that - near you] --> E[The listener] C[IYON / YON

Pointing with confidence

  • Ano ito? (What is this? — in my hand)
  • Magkano iyan? (How much is that? — near you)
  • Gusto ko iyon. (I want that one. — over there)

6. Comparisons — More, Less & The Most

Three little building blocks let you compare anything:

  • mas + adjective = "more…" → mas maganda (more beautiful), mas mabilis (faster)
  • kaysa (sa) = "than" → Mas matangkad siya kaysa sa akin. (He's taller than me.)
  • pinaka- + adjective = "the most…" → pinakamaganda (most beautiful), pinakamalaki (biggest)
  • kasing- + adjective = "as…as" → kasingtangkad (as tall as)

Putting it together

  • Mas mura ang gulay kaysa sa karne. (Vegetables are cheaper than meat.)
  • Siya ang pinakamatalino sa klase. (She's the smartest in the class.)
  • Kasingganda mo ang bulaklak. (The flower is as beautiful as you.)

Practice — Put the Glue to Work

Exercise 1: Have or have not

Fill in may, mayroon, or wala:

  • "Do you have money?" → "_____ ka bang pera?" (Answer: mayroon)
  • "I have a dog." → "_____ akong aso." (Answer: may)
  • "There's no one." → "_____ng tao." (Answer: wala)

Exercise 2: Pick the linker

Add na or -ng (write just the linker):

  • maganda + babae → maganda___ babae (Answer: ng)
  • mabait + tao → mabait ___ tao (Answer: na)
  • isa + mansanas → isa___ mansanas (Answer: ng)

Exercise 3: Right pronoun form

Choose the correct NG- or SA-pronoun:

  • "My house" → "Ang bahay _____." (Answer: ko)
  • "Give it to me" → "Ibigay mo _____." (Answer: sa akin)
  • "He loves me" → "Mahal _____ ako." (Answer: niya)

Exercise 4: The right particle

Fill the blank with a particle:

  • "I already ate." → "Kumain _____ ako." (Answer: na)
  • "Just one." → "Isa _____." (Answer: lang)
  • "Did you eat?" → "Kumain ka _____?" (Answer: ba)

Exercise 5: Compare them

  • "more beautiful" → "_____ maganda" (Answer: mas)
  • "the biggest" → "_____malaki" (Answer: pinaka)
  • "taller than me" → "mas matangkad _____ sa akin" (Answer: kaysa)

Where to Go From Here

These six patterns are the ones you'll reuse in almost every sentence you ever build. Notice them in Filipino songs, shows, and conversations — the linker gluing words, the particles adding warmth, may and wala everywhere. The grammar you just met is the difference between translating word-by-word and actually thinking in Tagalog.
