Mga Pandiwang Katulong - Modals & Pseudo-verbs

How to say what you want, need, can, and should do

Some of the most useful words in daily Tagalog aren't ordinary verbs at all. Pseudo-verbs — sometimes called "helper" or modal words — let you talk about wanting, needing, being able to, and having to. You'll reach for them in almost every real conversation: I want to eat, I need help, Can I go?, You should rest. The good news: they don't conjugate for tense the way normal verbs do — you learn each word once and reuse it.

1. The Big Five

WordMeaningEveryday use
gustowant / likeGusto ko iyan. (I want that.)
ayawdon't want / don't likeAyaw ko. (I don't want to.)
kailanganneedKailangan ko ng tulong. (I need help.)
pwede (maaari)can / may (allowed)Pwede ba? (Can I? / Is it okay?)
dapatshould / must / ought toDapat kang magpahinga. (You should rest.)

Maaari is simply the more formal, polite twin of pwede — same meaning.

2. The One Pattern You Need

Every pseudo-verb follows the same simple shape:

[pseudo-verb] + [who] + na/-ng + [plain verb]

  • Gusto kong kumain. (I want to eat.)
  • Kailangan niyang matulog. (He/She needs to sleep.)
  • Dapat tayong umalis. (We should leave.)

The little -ng gluing "who" to the verb is the same linker from Lesson 10 (ko + -ng → kong).

To talk about a thing instead of an action, use ng before the noun:

  • Gusto ko ng kape. (I want coffee.)
  • Kailangan namin ng tubig. (We need water.)
  • Ayaw niya ng gulay. (He/She doesn't like vegetables.)

3. The Pronoun Split — the one thing to get right

Here's the detail that separates textbook Tagalog from natural Tagalog. The "who" uses a different pronoun set depending on the word, drawn straight from the Lesson 10 table:

Gusto kong matulog. (I want to sleep.)Pwede akong matulog. (I can sleep.)
Kailangan mong kumain. (You need to eat.)Dapat kang kumain. (You should eat.)
Ayaw niyang umalis. (He doesn't want to leave.)Pwede siyang umalis. (He can leave.)
Memory hook: gusto, ayaw, kailangan take the same "ko/mo/niya" possessive-style pronouns you'd use for "my/your/his." pwede, maaari, dapat take the plain subject pronouns "ako/ka/siya." Say a few out loud and your ear will start choosing correctly.

4. Asking & Answering

Turn any of these into a question by dropping in ba (from Lesson 10):

  • Gusto mo bang kumain? — Oo, gusto ko. / Hindi, ayaw ko. (Do you want to eat? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.)
  • Pwede ba akong pumunta? — Oo, pwede. / Hindi pwede. (Can I go? — Yes, you can. / No, you can't.)
  • Kailangan mo ba ng tulong? — Oo, salamat. (Do you need help? — Yes, thanks.)

5. Making Them Negative (a preview)

Each word has a natural "no" form — you'll meet the full system in the next lesson:

PositiveNegative

Practice — Say What You Mean

Exercise 1: Pick the pronoun set

Fill in the correct pronoun for "I" (either ko or ako):

  • "I want to eat." → "Gusto _____ng kumain." (Answer: ko)
  • "I can go." → "Pwede _____ng pumunta." (Answer: ako)
  • "I need to sleep." → "Kailangan _____ng matulog." (Answer: ko)
  • "I should study." → "Dapat _____ng mag-aral." (Answer: ako)

Exercise 2: Choose the right pseudo-verb

Fill the blank with gusto, ayaw, kailangan, pwede, or dapat:

  • "I need water." → "_____ ko ng tubig." (Answer: kailangan)
  • "Can I ask?" → "_____ bang magtanong?" (Answer: pwede)
  • "I don't want coffee." → "_____ ko ng kape." (Answer: ayaw)
  • "You should rest." → "_____ kang magpahinga." (Answer: dapat)

Exercise 3: Thing or action?

Add ng (before a noun) or the linker -ng (before a verb — write just ng):

  • "I want coffee." → "Gusto ko ___ kape." (Answer: ng)
  • "I want to sleep." → "Gusto ko___ matulog." (Answer: ng)
  • "We need help." → "Kailangan namin ___ tulong." (Answer: ng)

Why This Lesson Matters

Wants, needs, ability, and obligation are the backbone of everyday talk — ordering food, asking permission, making plans, giving advice. Once gusto, ayaw, kailangan, pwede, and dapat feel automatic, you can express intention in almost any situation, even before your verb vocabulary is large.
