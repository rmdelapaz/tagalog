Mga Utos at Pakiusap - Requests & Commands

Asking people to do things — from blunt to beautifully polite

You can ask about things now (Lesson 13). This lesson lets you ask people to do things — pass the salt, close the door, help you, wait a moment. Tagalog has a lovely built-in "politeness dial," and the single most useful tool on it is the little prefix paki-, which turns almost any verb into "please do it."

1. Plain Commands

A direct command uses the verb's command form plus ka (you, the doer) or mo (you, when the focus is the thing acted on):

Doing an action (actor focus) — add ka:

  • Kumain ka. (Eat.)
  • Umupo ka. (Sit down.)
  • Tumayo ka. (Stand up.)
  • Halika. (Come here.) · Tara na! (Let's go! — casual)

Acting on a thing (object focus) — add mo:

  • Kainin mo. (Eat it.)
  • Isara mo ang pinto. (Close the door.)
  • Buksan mo ang ilaw. (Turn on the light.)
Careful: a bare command can sound bossy. To a stranger or elder you'll almost always soften it — that's what the rest of this lesson is for.

2. paki- — the "please do" prefix

Attach paki- to the front of a verb and you've made a polite request. The doer, if named, is mo:

  • Pakisara ang pinto. (Please close the door.)
  • Pakiabot mo ang asin. (Please pass the salt.)
  • Pakibuksan mo ang bintana. (Please open the window.)
  • Pakitulungan mo ako. (Please help me.)

Add po for respect and nga to soften even further:

  • Pakiabot po ng tubig. (Please pass some water — respectful.)
  • Pakisara nga ang pinto. (Please do close the door — gentle.)

3. Asking Permission & Favors

To ask whether you may do something, reach back for pwede / maaari (Lesson 11) plus ba:

  • Pwede ba akong humingi ng tubig? (May I ask for some water?)
  • Pwede bang pakibuksan ang bintana? (Could you please open the window?)
  • Maaari po bang makiraan? (May I please pass through? — formal)

Handy openers that mean "excuse me / sorry":

PhraseUse
Paumanhin poExcuse me (formal, getting attention)
Pasensya na poSorry / pardon me
Sandali lang poJust a moment, please
PakiusapPlease / a request (I'm asking a favor)

4. Telling Someone NOT To — huwag

The negative command comes straight from Lesson 12: use huwag, never hindi, for "don't."

  • Huwag kang umalis. (Don't leave.)
  • Huwag mong isara ang pinto. (Don't close the door.)
  • Huwag po. (Please don't. — respectful)

The Politeness Dial

The same request — "close the door" — climbs from blunt to very polite. Pick the rung that fits the person:

Practice — Make the Ask

Exercise 1: Add the paki- request

Complete the polite request (write just the missing word):

  • "Please pass the salt." → "_____ mo ang asin." (Answer: pakiabot)
  • "Please close the door." → "_____ ang pinto." (Answer: pakisara)
  • "Please help me." → "_____ mo ako." (Answer: pakitulungan)

Exercise 2: Command or "don't"?

Fill with ka, mo, or huwag:

  • "Sit down." → "Umupo _____." (Answer: ka)
  • "Eat it." → "Kainin _____." (Answer: mo)
  • "Don't leave." → "_____ kang umalis." (Answer: huwag)

Exercise 3: Ask permission politely

Fill in the blank:

  • "May I ask for water?" → "_____ ba akong humingi ng tubig?" (Answer: pwede)
  • "Excuse me (formal)." → "_____ po." (Answer: paumanhin)
  • "Please don't. (respectful)" → "Huwag _____." (Answer: po)

Why This Lesson Matters

In Filipino culture, how you ask matters as much as what you ask. The same words with paki-, po, and a smile open doors that a blunt command would close. You now have the full range — from a quick Tara na! among friends to a gracious Pwede po bang… with an elder. Together with question words, you can drive a whole conversation: ask about anything, and ask anyone to do anything, kindly.
