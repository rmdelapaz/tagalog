Salawikain at Bugtong

Karunungang-bayan — wisdom compressed into six words

Karunungang-bayan — folk wisdom — covers three related genres: salawikain (proverbs, which carry a moral), kasabihan (sayings, which need not), and bugtong (riddles). They belong together because they share one technique: say a concrete thing, mean an abstract one.

They are also a live register. A well-placed salawikain closes an argument in a way no amount of reasoning does.

1. The Shape of a Salawikain

Most proverbs are built from two balanced halves. The parallelism is the form — it is what makes them memorable and what marks them as proverbs rather than opinions.

Nasa Diyos ang awa,
nasa tao ang gawa.

Literally: Mercy is with God, action is with man.

Means: Pray, but do the work. Trust is not a substitute for effort.

Structure: identical frame (nasa ___ ang ___), two contrasting fillers. Notice the rhyme too: awa / gawa.

2. The Core Set

SalawikainLiteralMeans
Kung may tiyaga, may nilaga.If there is perseverance, there is stew.Hard work pays off.
Habang may buhay, may pag-asa.While there is life, there is hope.Don't give up yet.
Nasa Diyos ang awa, nasa tao ang gawa.Mercy with God, action with man.Pray, but act.
Ang hindi lumingon sa pinanggalingan ay hindi makararating sa paroroonan.One who does not look back at where they came from will not reach their destination.Honour your origins.
Aanhin pa ang damo kung patay na ang kabayo?What use is the grass when the horse is dead?Too little, too late.
Ang hindi marunong magmahal sa sariling wika ay mahigit pa sa hayop at malansang isda.One who cannot love their own language is worse than a beast and a rotten fish.Attributed to Rizal; the language-pride proverb.
The lumingon proverb is the most quoted of them all — at graduations, at homecomings, in political speeches, and on the walls of overseas-worker organisations. Notice that it is built on the ay-inversion of Lesson 3, with two matched pa- nouns: pinanggalingan (where one came from) and paroroonan (where one is going).

3. Bugtong — Riddles That Are Metaphors

A bugtong states an image; the answer is an ordinary object. They are short, almost always rhymed, and they trained exactly the figurative reading that Lesson 14 develops.

BugtongLiteralSagot
Baston ni Adan, hindi mabilang.Adam's canes, beyond counting.ulan (rain)
Isang balong malalim, puno ng patalim.A deep well full of blades.bibig (mouth)
Heto na si Kaka, bubuka-buka.Here comes Kaka, opening and opening.gunting (scissors)
Nagtago si Pedro, labas ang ulo.Pedro hid, but his head sticks out.pako (nail)
Buto't balat, lumilipad.Bone and skin, and it flies.saranggola (kite)

Riddles were a social game. Traditionally traded at wakes (lamay) to keep mourners awake and at gatherings as a contest of wit. The pleasure was never in the answer — it was in how far the image could travel before someone caught it.

GenreCarries a moral?Has an answer?Example

5. Using One Without Embarrassing Yourself

A salawikain is a closing move. It lands where an argument ends, as the thing that needs no further support.

✅ After a long discussion about a friend's persistence paying off: Talaga nga namang kung may tiyaga, may nilaga.

❌ Opening a work email with a proverb. It reads as a greeting card.

✅ At a graduation or homecoming: Huwag sana nating kalimutan — ang hindi lumingon sa pinanggalingan… (trailing off is normal; everyone can finish it).

Trailing off is a feature. Because the core proverbs are universally known, speakers routinely say only the first half. Finishing someone's salawikain for them is a small act of belonging.

Practice — Wisdom and Riddles

Exercise 1: Complete the salawikain

  • Kung may tiyaga, may _____. → (Answer: nilaga)
  • Habang may buhay, may _____. → (Answer: pag-asa)
  • Nasa Diyos ang awa, nasa tao ang _____. → (Answer: gawa)
  • Aanhin pa ang damo kung patay na ang _____? → (Answer: kabayo)

Exercise 2: What does it mean?

Answer in English, one or two words:

  • "Aanhin pa ang damo kung patay na ang kabayo?" → too _____ (Answer: late)
  • "Kung may tiyaga, may nilaga." → hard _____ pays off (Answer: work)

Exercise 3: Solve the bugtong

  • Isang balong malalim, puno ng patalim. → _____ (Answer: bibig)
  • Baston ni Adan, hindi mabilang. → _____ (Answer: ulan)
  • Heto na si Kaka, bubuka-buka. → _____ (Answer: gunting)
  • Nagtago si Pedro, labas ang ulo. → _____ (Answer: pako)

Exercise 4: Name the genre

Answer salawikain or bugtong:

  • It has a moral and no answer. → _____ (Answer: salawikain)
  • It has an answer and no moral. → _____ (Answer: bugtong)
  • "Buto't balat, lumilipad." → _____ (Answer: bugtong)

Exercise 5: Proverb vocabulary

  • to look back → _____ (Answer: lumingon)
  • where one came from → _____ (Answer: pinanggalingan)
  • where one is going → _____ (Answer: paroroonan)
  • folk wisdom (the whole genre) → _____ (Answer: karunungang-bayan)

Where This Takes You

Proverbs and riddles are figurative language with the training wheels on — you know a hidden meaning is coming. Lesson 14 removes the signal: talinghaga, pahiwatig, and the everyday indirection that Filipino conversation runs on, where nobody announces that something is being implied.
