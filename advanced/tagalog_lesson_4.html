Mga Sugnay at Pagpapalawak

How Tagalog stacks clause on clause — and how to take a long sentence apart

A C1 reader's real obstacle is not unknown words. It is a sentence that runs for four lines, with three na's and a kung in it, and no obvious place to start. This lesson gives you the vocabulary to describe such a sentence and the technique to dismantle it.

1. The Parts, Named

TagalogEnglishWhat it does
sugnayclauseA predicate with its arguments
pangunahing sugnaymain clauseStands alone; carries the sentence's assertion
sugnay na pantulongsubordinate clauseCannot stand alone; hangs off the main clause
pangatnigconjunctionThe word that joins them

2. na as "That" — the Complementizer

You know na/-ng as the linker that joins an adjective to a noun. It does a second job: it introduces a whole embedded statement.

Alam ko na aalis siya bukas. — I know that he's leaving tomorrow.

Sinabi niya na hindi siya pupunta. — She said that she isn't coming.

Naniniwala akong tama ka. — I believe that you're right. (vowel → -ng)

For an embedded question, Tagalog switches to kung:

Tinanong niya kung aalis ako. — He asked whether I was leaving.

Hindi ko alam kung sino ang tumawag. — I don't know who called.

Titingnan natin kung kaya natin. — We'll see whether we can.

The split to hold on to: a reported statement takes na; a reported question takes kung — even when the English sentence uses "that" for both. Hindi ko alam na… means "I didn't know that…"; Hindi ko alam kung… means "I don't know whether…". Different meanings entirely.

3. Clauses Becoming Noun Phrases

Wrap a clause in ang plus a head noun and it becomes something a verb can take as a subject or object. These frames are the backbone of essay Tagalog:

FrameExampleEnglish
ang katotohanang…Ang katotohanang mahirap ito ay hindi dahilan.The fact that this is hard is not a reason.
ang dahilan kung bakit…Ang dahilan kung bakit siya umalis ay simple.The reason why he left is simple.
ang posibilidad na…Tinalakay nila ang posibilidad na magbago ito.They discussed the possibility that this would change.
ang paraan kung paano…Ipinaliwanag niya ang paraan kung paano ito ginawa.She explained the way in which it was done.

Note how these combine with Lesson 3: a clause-turned-noun-phrase is exactly the kind of heavy subject that wants ay after it.

4. The Subordinators

WordMeaningExample
upangin order to (formal)Nag-aral siya upang makapasa.
nangso that / whenNagmadali kami nang hindi mahuli.
maliban kungunlessTuloy ang lakad maliban kung umulan.
hangga'tas long as / untilMaghihintay ako hangga't kailangan.
sa halip nainstead ofNaglakad siya sa halip na sumakay.
para bangas if / as thoughKumilos siya para bang walang nangyari.
sa kabila ng katotohanangdespite the fact thatTumuloy sila sa kabila ng katotohanang delikado ito.
Upang vs nang. Both give purpose. Upang belongs to writing and formal speech; nang is the everyday choice and also does duty as "when". In an essay, upang. In conversation, nang — or just para.

5. Taking a Long Sentence Apart

The technique is always the same: find the main predicate first. In Tagalog it is usually the very first word or phrase of the sentence — or, in an inverted sentence, the phrase right after ay. Everything else is attached material.

graph TD S["A long sentence"] --> I["Is there an 'ay'?"] I -->|yes| P1["Main predicate = right after 'ay'<br/>Subject = everything before it"] I -->|no| P2["Main predicate = the opening phrase"] P1 --> N["Strip off each na / kung / upang clause"] P2 --> N N --> R["Read the bare main clause<br/>then add the pieces back"] style R fill:#8e44ad,color:#fff

"Ipinaliwanag ng guro na ang dahilan kung bakit mahalaga ang wika ay dahil ito ang nagdadala ng kultura, bagaman hindi ito laging napapansin ng mga mag-aaral."

Main predicate: Ipinaliwanag ng guro — "The teacher explained…"

What was explained (the na clause): ang dahilan kung bakit mahalaga ang wika ay dahil ito ang nagdadala ng kultura — "the reason language matters is that it carries culture".

The trailing concession: bagaman hindi ito laging napapansin ng mga mag-aaral — "although students don't always notice it".

Plain reading: The teacher explained that language matters because it carries culture, though students don't always notice.

Practice — Building and Unpacking

Exercise 1: na or kung?

Fill the blank:

  • Alam ko _____ aalis siya bukas. → (Answer: na)
  • Tinanong niya _____ aalis ako. → (Answer: kung)
  • Hindi ko alam _____ sino ang tumawag. → (Answer: kung)
  • Sinabi niya _____ hindi siya pupunta. → (Answer: na)

Exercise 2: Complete the noun-phrase frame

  • Ang _____ mahirap ito ay hindi dahilan. (the fact that) → (Answer: katotohanang)
  • Ang _____ kung bakit siya umalis ay simple. (the reason) → (Answer: dahilan)
  • Tinalakay nila ang _____ na magbago ito. (the possibility) → (Answer: posibilidad)

Exercise 3: Choose the subordinator

Use upang, maliban kung, hangga't, sa halip na, or para bang:

  • Nag-aral siya _____ makapasa. → (Answer: upang)
  • Tuloy ang lakad _____ umulan. → (Answer: maliban kung)
  • Naglakad siya _____ sumakay. → (Answer: sa halip na)
  • Kumilos siya _____ walang nangyari. → (Answer: para bang)

Exercise 4: Name the part

Give the Tagalog term — sugnay, pangunahing sugnay, sugnay na pantulong, or pangatnig:

  • the clause that can stand alone → _____ (Answer: pangunahing sugnay)
  • the joining word itself → _____ (Answer: pangatnig)
  • the clause that cannot stand alone → _____ (Answer: sugnay na pantulong)

Exercise 5: Find the main predicate

Give the first word of the main clause:

  • Ipinaliwanag ng guro na mahalaga ang wika. → _____ (Answer: ipinaliwanag)
  • Ang edukasyon ay karapatan ng bawat bata. → _____ (Answer: karapatan)
  • Naniniwala kami na magbabago ang lahat. → _____ (Answer: naniniwala)

Where This Takes You

Long sentences are no longer a wall — they are a main clause with luggage. What they carry, though, is often hedged: marahil, tila, diumano. Lesson 5 takes up the two questions every careful Tagalog sentence answers about itself: how sure am I, and how do I know?
