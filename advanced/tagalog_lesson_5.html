Gaano Ka Katiyak, at Paano Mo Nalaman?

The certainty ladder, and marking where your information came from

Most learners get to B2 with one word for uncertainty: siguro. Tagalog has at least ten, arranged on a fine scale, and a second, separate system for saying where you got the information. Careful Tagalog answers both questions at once, and news Tagalog is built almost entirely out of this machinery.

1. Two Different Questions

SystemThe question it answersWords
ModalityHow confident am I?tiyak, malamang, marahil, tila, maaaring, baka, siguro, yata
EvidentialityHow do I know?daw/raw, ayon kay/sa, anang, diumano, sabi-sabi

They are independent. You can be very sure of something you heard second-hand, or doubtful about something you saw yourself.

3. The Three That Trip People Up

baka carries worry

Baka is not neutral. It leans toward something you would rather avoid, which is why it doubles as a warning.

Baka mahulog ka. — You might fall. (= careful!)

Baka wala siya. — He might not be there. (= and that would be a problem)

For a neutral guess, use siguro or malamang.

yata is the softest hedge, and it sits after the word it doubts

Tama yata. — I think that's right.

Umalis na yata siya. — He's left already, I think.

Yata is an enclitic: it follows the first full word of the phrase, exactly like na, pa, and daw.

marahil and tila belong to the page

In a formal paragraph they read as measured. In casual chat they read as bookish — the difference between "perhaps" and "maybe" in English, but sharper.

Essay: Marahil ay nagmula ito sa lumang kaugalian.

Chat: Siguro galing 'yan sa lumang ugali.

Diumano is a journalist's shield. Philippine news uses it to report an accusation without asserting it — the exact function of "allegedly". You will see it in almost every crime report. It is not a casual word: dropping it into conversation sounds like you are reading a police blotter aloud.
Anang is a literary speech tag. Older fiction and translations use ani / anang where modern prose would use sabi ni. You will meet it again in Lesson 12.

5. Stacking the Two Systems

Because they answer different questions, both can appear in one sentence — a probability about a report.

Marahil ay umalis na raw siya. — He has probably left, or so they say.

Tila totoo ang diumano'y natuklasan nila. — What they allegedly discovered seems to be true.

Malamang, ayon sa ulat, tumaas ang presyo. — According to the report, prices have most likely risen.

The order is flexible, but the enclitics (daw/raw, yata) always stay glued to the first full word of their phrase.
