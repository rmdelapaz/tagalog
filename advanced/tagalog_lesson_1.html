Ang Buong Talaan ng Pokus

The two focus types the intermediate course never reached — and the full six-way system

At B1 you learned four focuses: actor (-um-/mag-), object (-in), locative (-an), and benefactive (i-). That was enough to speak. It is not enough to read. Filipino writing, and careful Filipino speech, uses two more: instrument focus (ipang-) and causal focus (ika-).

This lesson completes the inventory — and gives you the Tagalog names for the roles, so that from here on you can read about Tagalog in Tagalog.

1. The Six Roles, in Tagalog

Every focus type spotlights one participant. Here is the whole set, with the terms a Filipino grammar book would use:

Role (English)Tagalog termFocus affixExample verb
Doer / actortagaganap-um- / mag-humiwa (cut)
Thing acted onlayon-inhiwain (be cut)
Place of the actionganapan-anhiwaan (be cut on)
Recipienttagatanggap-anbigyan (be given to)
Beneficiarytagatanggap ng pakinabangi-ihiwa (cut for someone)
Instrument / toolkasangkapanipang-ipanghiwa (cut with)
Causesanhiika-ikatuwa (be glad because of)
Why the terms matter. Filipino textbooks, KWF materials, and university grammars all use tagaganap, layon, ganapan, kasangkapan, and sanhi. Knowing them is the difference between reading a Tagalog grammar explanation and needing it translated for you.

2. Instrument Focus — ipang-

Instrument focus puts ang on the tool. The doer drops to a ng-pronoun, exactly as in Lesson 1 of the intermediate course.

Conjugating ipanghiwa (hiwa — to slice)

AspectFormMeaning
Infinitiveipanghiwato use for slicing
Completedipinanghiwawas used for slicing
Incompletedipinanghihiwais being used for slicing
Contemplatedipanghihiwawill be used for slicing

The pattern: ipa → ipina (completed), with reduplication of the root's first syllable for the two ongoing/future aspects. It is the i- conjugation you already know, carried over onto a pang- stem.

Ipinanghiwa ko ng karne ang kutsilyo. — The knife is what I cut the meat with.

Ipinambayad niya ang huling pera niya. — His last money is what he paid with.

Ipangsusulat ko ang bagong panulat. — The new pen is what I will write with.

Compare the actor-focus version of the same event: Humiwa ako ng karne gamit ang kutsilyo — "I cut meat using the knife." Both are correct. The ipang- version is what you use when the conversation is about the knife.

3. The pang- Noun Standing Beside It

From one root, Tagalog builds both a verb and a tool-name:

Rootipang- verb (use it to…)pang- noun (the tool)
hiwa (slice)ipanghiwapanghiwa — a cutting implement
sulat (write)ipangsulatpanulat — a writing implement, a pen
bura (erase)ipamburapambura — an eraser
bayad (pay)ipambayadpambayad — the money for paying
Notice panulat, not *pangsulat. The pang- prefix changes shape depending on the sound that follows it, and often swallows the root's first consonant. That is the whole subject of Lesson 2 — for now, just register that the noun and the verb are siblings.

4. Causal Focus — ika-

Causal focus puts ang on the cause: the thing that produced a feeling or an outcome.

Conjugating ikatuwa (tuwa — delight)

AspectFormMeaning
Infinitiveikatuwato be the cause of gladness
Completedikinatuwawas the cause of gladness
Incompletedikinatutuwais the cause of gladness
Contemplatedikatutuwawill be the cause of gladness

Ikinatuwa ko ang balita. — The news is what made me glad.

Ikinagalit niya ang sinabi ko. — What I said is what angered him.

Ikinalungkot namin ang pag-alis mo. — Your leaving is what saddened us.

Ikinabuhay ng pamilya ang maliit na tindahan. — The little shop is what the family lived on.

ika- verbs cluster around emotion and life outcome. The common set is small and worth memorising whole:

5. Choosing Among Six

The rule has not changed since B1: put ang on whatever the conversation is about. There are simply more options now.

graph TD Q["What is this sentence about?"] --> A["The doer<br/>-um- / mag-"] Q --> B["The thing acted on<br/>-in"] Q --> C["The place or recipient<br/>-an"] Q --> D["The person it's for<br/>i-"] Q --> E["The tool used<br/>ipang-"] Q --> F["The cause<br/>ika-"] style E fill:#8e44ad,color:#fff style F fill:#c0392b,color:#fff

One event, six tellings — a man slices meat with a knife for his mother, and it makes her happy:

Humiwa siya ng karne. (doer) · Hiniwa niya ang karne. (thing) · Hiniwaan niya ang lamesa. (place) · Ipinanghiwa niya ang kutsilyo. (tool) · Ikinatuwa ng nanay niya ang ginawa niya. (cause)

Practice — The Full Paradigm

Exercise 1: Name the role

Give the Tagalog term for the role in focus — tagaganap, layon, ganapan, kasangkapan, or sanhi:

  • Ipinambayad niya ang pera. → _____ (Answer: kasangkapan)
  • Ikinagalit ko ang balita. → _____ (Answer: sanhi)
  • Kumain siya ng tinapay. → _____ (Answer: tagaganap)
  • Kinain niya ang tinapay. → _____ (Answer: layon)

Exercise 2: Build the instrument-focus verb

Give the completed ipang- form:

  • hiwa → _____ (Answer: ipinanghiwa)
  • bayad → _____ (Answer: ipinambayad)
  • sulat → _____ (Answer: ipinangsulat)

Exercise 3: The tool-name beside it

Give the pang- noun for the tool:

  • the implement you slice with → _____ (Answer: panghiwa)
  • the implement you write with → _____ (Answer: panulat)
  • the thing you erase with → _____ (Answer: pambura)

Exercise 4: Causal focus

Give the completed ika- form:

  • tuwa → _____ (Answer: ikinatuwa)
  • lungkot → _____ (Answer: ikinalungkot)
  • matay → _____ (Answer: ikinamatay)

Exercise 5: Which affix?

Choose the focus affix — write ipang- or ika-:

  • The spotlight is on the hammer you used. → _____ (Answer: ipang-)
  • The spotlight is on the letter that made her cry. → _____ (Answer: ika-)
  • The spotlight is on the money you paid with. → _____ (Answer: ipang-)

Where This Takes You

The focus system is now complete. Nothing in the rest of this course will introduce a seventh focus, because there isn't one — everything else you meet will be a combination or a register choice built on these six. What comes next is the machinery underneath pang-: why sulat gives panulat and bili gives mamili. That is Lesson 2, and it turns a long list of memorised forms into three short rules.
