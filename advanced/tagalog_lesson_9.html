Retorika at Panghihikayat

Argument with a shape — position, proof, concession, refutation, conclusion

Intermediate Lesson 19 gave you the moves: agree, disagree, hedge, persuade. This lesson arranges them. A C1 argument is not a pile of opinions with connectors between them — it has an architecture, and Filipino audiences hear that architecture as clearly as they hear the words.

1. The Five-Part Shape

Pagpapabulaan<br/>refute it anyway"] D --> E["5. Kongklusyon<br/>land the conclusion"] style A fill:#8e44ad,color:#fff style E fill:#16a085,color:#fff

Skipping step 3 is the commonest mistake advanced learners make. In Filipino argumentation, conceding first is not weakness — it is the move that earns you the right to refute.

2. Stating the Position

FrameEnglishForce
Naniniwala ako na…I believe that…personal
Sa aking paninindigan…It is my position that…formal, committed
Nararapat na…It ought to be that…normative
Ang mahalaga ay…What matters is…pseudo-cleft, Lesson 3

Sa aking paninindigan, dapat ituring na karapatan — hindi pribilehiyo — ang edukasyon.

3. Escalating the Support

Good support does not repeat; it climbs. Each connector signals that the next point is stronger than the last.

ConnectorEnglishPosition in the ladder
Una sa lahat…First of all…opening point
Bukod dito…Moreover / besides this…an added point
Higit pa rito…Furthermore…a stronger point
Sa katunayan…In fact…the clinching evidence

Una sa lahat, mura ang hakbang na ito. Bukod dito, mabilis itong maipatutupad. Higit pa rito, napatunayan na itong epektibo sa ibang bansa. Sa katunayan, bumaba ang bilang nang wala pang isang taon.

4. Concede, Then Refute

The single most useful structure in Filipino argument:

Totoo nga na… ngunit…

"It is indeed true that… but…" — the nga is doing real work. It grants the point sincerely, which is what makes the reversal land.

Totoo nga na magastos ang panukala, ngunit mas magastos ang hindi kumilos. — It's true the measure is expensive, but doing nothing is more expensive.

Maaaring totoo na mahirap ito sa umpisa, subalit sulit ang bunga.

Kahit na may mga pagkukulang, hindi maikakaila ang naging tagumpay.

5. Rhetorical Moves

The rhetorical question

A question that asserts. Filipino oratory uses these heavily, and they are far more common in Tagalog persuasion than in English.

Hindi ba't tayo rin ang magdurusa? — Aren't we the ones who will suffer? (= we will)

Sino ba ang makikinabang dito? — Who exactly benefits from this? (= nobody deserving)

Hanggang kailan tayo maghihintay? — How long are we to wait? (= too long already)

Parallelism

Repeating a structure across clauses. This is the most recognisable feature of persuasive Filipino — and it carries even further aloud than on the page.

Hindi ito usapin ng pera. Hindi ito usapin ng panahon. Usapin ito ng dangal.

Para sa bata. Para sa magulang. Para sa bayan.

6. Landing the Conclusion

CloserEnglishTone
Kung gayon…If so / therefore…logical
Sa kabuuan…On the whole…summative
Samakatuwid…Therefore…formal
Nararapat lamang na…It is only right that…moral
Dapat nating tandaan…We must remember…exhortative

Nararapat lamang na ituring nating karapatan ang edukasyon. Dapat nating tandaan na ang pamumuhunan sa kabataan ay pamumuhunan sa bayan.

Practice — Building an Argument

Exercise 1: Name the stage

Answer paninindigan, patunay, pagkilala, or kongklusyon:

  • "Sa aking paninindigan, dapat ituring itong karapatan." → _____ (Answer: paninindigan)
  • "Totoo nga na magastos ito…" → _____ (Answer: pagkilala)
  • "Bumaba ang bilang nang wala pang isang taon." → _____ (Answer: patunay)
  • "Nararapat lamang na kumilos tayo ngayon." → _____ (Answer: kongklusyon)

Exercise 2: Climb the ladder

Put the right connector in:

  • _____ sa lahat, mura ang hakbang. (first of all) → (Answer: una)
  • _____ dito, mabilis itong maipatutupad. (moreover) → (Answer: bukod)
  • Higit pa _____, napatunayan na ito. (furthermore) → (Answer: rito)
  • Sa _____, bumaba ang bilang. (in fact) → (Answer: katunayan)

Exercise 3: Concede, then refute

  • Totoo _____ na magastos ito, ngunit… (indeed) → (Answer: nga)
  • Maaaring totoo na mahirap, _____ sulit ang bunga. (but, formal) → (Answer: subalit)
  • Kahit na may pagkukulang, hindi _____ ang tagumpay. (cannot be denied) → (Answer: maikakaila)

Exercise 4: Rhetorical or genuine question?

  • "Hindi ba't tayo rin ang magdurusa?" → _____ (Answer: rhetorical)
  • "Anong oras ang pulong bukas?" → _____ (Answer: genuine)
  • "Hanggang kailan tayo maghihintay?" → _____ (Answer: rhetorical)

Exercise 5: Close it

  • _____ lamang na kumilos tayo. (it is only right) → (Answer: nararapat)
  • _____ nating tandaan ang aral na ito. (we must remember) → (Answer: dapat)
  • Sa _____, malinaw ang dahilan. (on the whole) → (Answer: kabuuan)

Where This Takes You

You can now build a case in Tagalog that a Filipino audience will recognise as an argument rather than a list. Lesson 10 closes C1 by widening your ear: everything so far has assumed Manila Tagalog, and most Tagalog speakers do not live in Manila.
