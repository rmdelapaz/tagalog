Pagsasalin at ang Di-Maisasalin

The craft of translation — and the words that refuse to cross

Translation is the most demanding thing you can do with two languages, because it forces you to be exact about what you actually understood. At C2 the skill is not producing a translation — it is naming the compromise you made and defending it.

This lesson covers the two structural traps that catch every English-speaking translator of Tagalog, then the words that have no English at all.

2. Trap One — Focus Is Not Voice

English has active and passive. Tagalog has focus. They are not the same system, and treating object focus as a passive produces stiff, wrong English.

Kinain ko ang tinapay.

❌ "The bread was eaten by me." — grammatical English, terrible translation. Tagalog object focus is not a passive; it is the ordinary, unmarked way to say this when the bread is known.

✅ "I ate the bread." — the definite article carries what ang was carrying.

TagalogBad (mirrors the structure)Good (carries the meaning)
Kumain ako ng tinapay.I ate bread.I ate some bread. / I had bread.
Kinain ko ang tinapay.The bread was eaten by me.I ate the bread.
Ipinanghiwa ko ang kutsilyo.The knife was cut-with by me.I used the knife to cut it.

Going the other way is just as treacherous: an English passive usually wants Tagalog object focus, but an English active with a definite object also wants object focus. Voice does not map onto focus at all — definiteness does.

3. Trap Two — Tense Is Not Aspect

English marks when. Tagalog marks how complete. The overlap is partial and the gaps are where translations go wrong.

EnglishTagalogNote
I ate.Kumain ako.Completed — clean match
I was eating.Kumakain ako.Incompleted — clean match
I eat (habitually).Kumakain ako.Same form as "was eating" — English splits, Tagalog doesn't
I had eaten.Kumain na ako. / Katatapos ko lang kumain.No perfect aspect — you need na or a time anchor
I have just eaten.Katatapos ko lang kumain.The recently-completed form (B1 Lesson 7)
The translator's habit: when English uses a perfect tense, stop and ask what the Tagalog needs — usually na, pa, a katatapos, or an explicit time word. Copying the tense produces Tagalog that is grammatical and subtly wrong.

4. The Untranslatables

These are not merely "hard" words. Each packages a feeling plus a social situation, and English has to unpack both.

WordClosest EnglishWhat English loses
kiliggiddy thrillIt is physical — a shiver — and specifically romantic.
tamposulking"Sulking" is childish and unattractive. Tampo is legitimate withdrawal that invites coaxing (lambing) — it is part of how affection works, not a failure of it.
gigil—The clenched, almost aggressive urge produced by something unbearably cute.
lambingaffectionAffection actively shown, often to dissolve someone's tampo.
hiyashame / embarrassmentAlso propriety and social face. Which English word fits depends entirely on the sentence.
utang na loobdebt of gratitudeThe English is transactional and settleable. The Tagalog is relational, open-ended, and binds both parties.
sayangwhat a waste / what a shameA single interjection in Tagalog, a whole phrase in English — and it covers regret for a lost opportunity specifically.
bahala nacome what mayNot fatalism and not recklessness: it is committing to act despite uncertainty, having done what you could.
pakikiramdamsensing / reading the roomA deliberate social skill — feeling out a situation before speaking. The whole apparatus of Lesson 14 sits inside this word.
What to actually do with them. Three options, and the choice is yours to defend: borrow the word and gloss it once ("tampo — a kind of affectionate withdrawal"); unpack it into a phrase; or substitute the nearest English and accept the loss. For literature, borrow. For subtitles, substitute. For scholarship, unpack.

5. A Worked Translation

Tagalog: "Nagtampo siya noong hindi ko siya sinundo, pero katatapos ko lang mag-lambing kaya ayos na kami. Utang na loob ko naman sa kanya ang lahat."

Literal na salin: "She sulked when I did not fetch her, but I just finished being affectionate so we are fine. My debt of gratitude to her is everything."

Malayang salin: "She went quiet on me when I didn't pick her up, but I've just made it up to her and we're fine now. I owe her everything, really."

The compromises: nagtampo → "went quiet on me" (keeps the relational flavour, loses the invitation to be coaxed); mag-lambing → "made it up to her" (loses the tenderness); katatapos ko lang → "I've just" (the recently-completed aspect maps cleanly here); utang na loob → "I owe her everything" (loses the bindingness, keeps the weight).
