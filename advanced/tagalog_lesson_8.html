Wika ng Batas, Pamahalaan at Balita

The most compressed register Filipino has — and how to read it

Open a Philippine newspaper's Filipino section, or a copy of a city ordinance, and the language changes character. Sentences shorten. Actors vanish. Verbs cluster in one shape. This is not a different dialect — it is the same grammar under maximum compression, and once you see the pattern it stops being difficult.

1. Why Everything Is in Object Focus

Official Filipino puts the law, the order, or the decision in focus, not the person behind it. That means object-focus verbs (Intermediate Lesson 1) almost everywhere, with the actor demoted to a ng-phrase — or dropped.

Ipinatupad ng pamahalaan ang bagong patakaran. — The government implemented the new policy.

Ipinatupad ang bagong patakaran. — The new policy was implemented. (by whom? unstated — and that is deliberate)

Kinatigan ng korte ang petisyon. — The court upheld the petition.

This is not the passive voice. English would call these passives; Tagalog object focus is a fully transitive construction with the object in the spotlight. The effect resembles a passive — the actor becomes optional — which is precisely why institutions favour it.

5. Headline Grammar

Headlines compress further still: markers vanish, and the reader supplies the missing ang.

HeadlineFull sentenceEnglish
Bagong batas, ipinatupad naIpinatupad na ang bagong batas.New law now in force
Petisyon ng grupo, ibinasuraIbinasura ang petisyon ng grupo.Group's petition dismissed
Presyo ng bigas, tumaasTumaas ang presyo ng bigas.Rice prices rise

The pattern is consistent: topic, comma, bare verb. The comma is doing the job of the marker.

Diumano earns its keep here. Lesson 5's "allegedly" is a legal necessity in crime reporting: Diumano ay nagnakaw ang suspek reports the accusation without the paper asserting it. Expect it in nearly every crime story.

6. A Worked News Item

"Ibinasura kahapon ng hukuman ang sakdal laban sa tatlong nasasakdal, matapos umanong kulangin sa ebidensiya ang panig ng nagsasakdal. Ayon sa hatol, walang sapat na batayan upang ituloy ang usapin. Isinusulong naman ng grupo ang panibagong panukala hinggil sa karapatang pantao."

Sentence 1: main verb ibinasura (was dismissed) — subject: ang sakdal. Actor: ng hukuman. Reason clause introduced by matapos umanong ("after reportedly…").

Sentence 2: Ayon sa hatol cites the ruling; the claim is that there was no sufficient basis (batayan) to continue.

Sentence 3: Isinusulong — ongoing aspect: the group is pushing a new measure, hinggil sa human rights (Lesson 3's formal connector).

Plain reading: The court dismissed the charge against three defendants yesterday after the prosecution reportedly lacked evidence. The ruling found no sufficient basis to proceed. Meanwhile, the group is pushing a new human-rights measure.

Umano and diumano are the same hedge; umano attaches after the word it qualifies, diumano tends to open the clause.

Practice — Reading the Official Register

Exercise 1: Legal vocabulary

  • constitution → _____ (Answer: saligang batas)
  • court → _____ (Answer: hukuman)
  • the accused → _____ (Answer: nasasakdal)
  • verdict → _____ (Answer: hatol)

Exercise 2: What does the verb mean?

  • ibinasura → _____ (Answer: dismissed)
  • kinatigan → _____ (Answer: upheld)
  • inihain → _____ (Answer: filed)

Exercise 3: Un-compress the headline

Give the marker the headline dropped:

  • "Bagong batas, ipinatupad na" → Ipinatupad na _____ bagong batas. (Answer: ang)
  • "Presyo ng bigas, tumaas" → Tumaas _____ presyo ng bigas. (Answer: ang)

Exercise 4: Which focus?

Answer actor or object:

  • Ipinatupad ng pamahalaan ang patakaran. → _____ (Answer: object)
  • Nagsalita ang pinuno kahapon. → _____ (Answer: actor)
  • Kinatigan ng korte ang petisyon. → _____ (Answer: object)

Exercise 5: The news hedge

  • _____ ay nagnakaw ang suspek. (allegedly, opening the clause) → (Answer: diumano)
  • matapos _____ kulangin sa ebidensiya (reportedly, attached after the word) → (Answer: umanong)
  • _____ sa ulat, tumaas ang bilang. (according to) → (Answer: ayon)

Where This Takes You

Law and news are Filipino at its most impersonal. Lesson 9 swings to the opposite pole: the register in which a speaker is unmistakably present, taking a position and trying to move you.
