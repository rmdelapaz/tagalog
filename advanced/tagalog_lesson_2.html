Ang Panlaping paN-

Why bili becomes mamili — three rules that replace a hundred memorised words

Lesson 1 left you with a puzzle: sulat gives panulat, but hugas gives panghugas and bura gives pambura. Three different prefixes? No — one prefix, written paN-, whose final nasal takes its shape from the sound that follows it. The same happens to maN- on verbs.

Learn the rule once and you stop memorising these forms. You generate them.

1. The Assimilation Rule

The nasal at the end of paN- / maN- moves to where the next sound is made in the mouth. This part is exceptionless:

If the root starts with…paN- becomesmaN- becomesExample
p, b, m (lips)pam-mam-bili → mamili
d, l, r, s, t, n (tongue-tip)pan-man-tahi → manahi
a, e, i, o, u, k, g, h, w, y, ng (back / open)pang-mang-isda → mangisda

2. The Second Half: the Root Loses a Consonant

Assimilation is only half the story. In most words, the root's own first consonant then disappears:

RootLiterallyActual formMeaning
bilimaN + bilimamilito shop / to choose
punasmaN + punasmamunasto wipe (habitually)
putolpaN + putolpamutola cutting tool
tahimaN + tahimanahito sew (as work)
sulatpaN + sulatpanulata pen
kuhamaN + kuhamanguhato gather / collect

Deletion is usual after p, b, t, s, k. It never happens after l, r, m, n, ng, h, w, y, g, or a vowel:

RootFormWhy the consonant stays
hugaspanghugash never deletes
hirammanghiramh never deletes
gamotmanggamotg never deletes
isdamangisdavowel-initial — nothing to delete
The honest caveat. A handful of common words keep their consonant even though the rule predicts deletion: pambura (not *pamura, which would collide with mura), pambayad, pambili alongside pamili. Treat deletion as the default and these as learned exceptions — that is exactly how native speakers hold them.

3. What the Prefixes Produce

PatternGives youExamples
paN- + rootthe toolpanulat (pen), panghugas (dish soap), pamunas (rag), pantahi (needle & thread)
maN- + rootthe habitual / occupational verbmamili (shop), mangisda (fish for a living), manahi (sew as work)
maN- + reduplicationthe person who does itmangingisda (fisherman), manunulat (writer), manggagamot (healer), mandurukot (pickpocket)

Mamimili ako sa palengke bukas. — I'll do the shopping at the market tomorrow.

Ang tatay niya ay mangingisda sa Batangas. — His father is a fisherman in Batangas.

Wala akong panulat; may pambura lang ako. — I have no pen; I only have an eraser.

4. ma-…-an — Perception That Arrives

This circumfix marks something you came to know, feel, or manage — not something you set out to do.

RootFormCompletedMeaning
tandamatandaannatandaanto manage to remember
intindimaintindihannaintindihanto grasp / understand
ramdammaramdamannaramdamanto feel / perceive
alammalamannalamanto find out

Naintindihan ko ang sinabi mo. — I understood what you said.

Naramdaman niya ang lindol. — She felt the earthquake.

Hindi ko natandaan ang pangalan niya. — I couldn't remember his name.

Compare the deliberate versions: tandaan mo ("make a point of remembering") is an instruction; natandaan ko is something that happened in your head.

5. pa-…-in and pa-…-an

Intermediate Lesson 4 gave you magpa- and ipa- for having things done. These two are the close relatives:

PatternMeaningExamples
pa- + root + -inmake someone do itpakainin (feed), painumin (give a drink), patulugin (put to sleep), paupuin (seat someone)
pa- + root + -andirect the action at someonepahiraman (lend to), pabilhan (buy for/from), pautangan (extend credit to)

Pakainin mo ang aso bago tayo umalis. — Feed the dog before we go.

Pinahiraman niya ako ng libro. — She lent me a book.

6. Becoming and Coming to Have

VerbMeaningAspects
magingto becomenaging / nagiging / magiging
magkaroonto come to havenagkaroon / nagkakaroon / magkakaroon
magka- + rootto fall into a statemagkasakit, magkapera, magkaanak

Naging guro siya pagkatapos ng kolehiyo. — He became a teacher after college.

Nagkaroon kami ng pagkakataong magkita. — We had a chance to meet.

Nagkasakit ang nanay ko noong nakaraang taon. — My mother fell ill last year.

Practice — Generate, Don't Memorise

Exercise 1: Which shape of paN-?

Write pam-, pan-, or pang-:

  • before the root putol → _____ (Answer: pam-)
  • before the root sulat → _____ (Answer: pan-)
  • before the root hugas → _____ (Answer: pang-)
  • before the root isda → _____ (Answer: pang-)

Exercise 2: Apply both halves of the rule

Give the finished maN- verb:

  • maN + bili → _____ (Answer: mamili)
  • maN + tahi → _____ (Answer: manahi)
  • maN + hiram → _____ (Answer: manghiram)
  • maN + isda → _____ (Answer: mangisda)

Exercise 3: Name the doer

Give the maN- + reduplication noun:

  • one who fishes for a living → _____ (Answer: mangingisda)
  • one who writes → _____ (Answer: manunulat)
  • one who picks pockets → _____ (Answer: mandurukot)

Exercise 4: ma-…-an

Give the completed form:

  • intindi → _____ (Answer: naintindihan)
  • ramdam → _____ (Answer: naramdaman)
  • tanda → _____ (Answer: natandaan)

Exercise 5: Causing and becoming

Fill in the missing verb:

  • _____ mo ang bata bago matulog. (feed him) → (Answer: pakainin)
  • _____ siyang doktor noong 2019. (became) → (Answer: naging)
  • _____ kami ng bagong bahay. (came to have) → (Answer: nagkaroon)
