Ang Talumpati

Structure, occasion, and delivery — Tagalog on its feet

A talumpati is a formal speech, and Filipino audiences have firm expectations of one. They expect a ceremonial opening before a personal one. They expect parallelism. They expect to be told when the ending has begun. A speech that violates these feels, to a Filipino listener, unfinished — even if every sentence in it was correct.

This is where the whole course converges: Lesson 3's inversion, Lesson 6's deep vocabulary, Lesson 9's argument, Lesson 13's proverbs, all out loud.

1. The Three Parts

PartContains
PambungadGreeting → address to the panauhing pandangal and audience → thanks → what you are here to say
KatawanTwo or three points, each with an example, a story, or a figure
PangwakasThe signal (Bilang pangwakas…) → restate → call to action → closing thanks

2. Opening Ceremonially

The opening is formulaic on purpose. Filipino audiences read the formula as respect; improvising past it reads as presumption.

"Magandang hapon po sa inyong lahat.

Sa ating panauhing pandangal, sa mga guro, sa mga magulang, at sa mga mag-aaral na narito ngayon — isang mapagpalang hapon po.

Nais ko pong pasalamatan ang mga nag-anyaya sa akin upang makapagsalita sa inyong harapan."

Address formUse
Mga minamahal kong kababayanCivic, national, political — high ceremony
Mga kaibiganWarm, semi-formal
Mga guro, magulang, at mag-aaralSchool occasions — name each group present
Sa ating panauhing pandangalAlways first if a guest of honour is present
Name the groups. Filipino ceremonial openings enumerate who is in the room — officials, teachers, parents, students, guests — in descending order of formal precedence. Omitting a group that is present is noticed.

3. The Set Phrases

PhraseEnglishWhere
Sa ngalan ng…On behalf of…Opening, when representing a group
Nais ko pong pasalamatan…I wish to thank…Opening and closing
Nais ko pong ibahagi…I wish to share…Introducing your thesis
Kung inyong mamarapatin…If you will permit me…Before a digression or a bold claim
Bilang pangwakas…In closing…The ending signal
Maraming salamat po at magandang araw.Thank you very much and good day.The final line
Bilang pangwakas is not optional. It tells the audience the speech is ending, which lets them prepare to applaud. Without it, a Filipino audience is left uncertain whether you have finished — the most common way a foreigner's talumpati lands awkwardly.

5. Delivery

Parallelism carries further aloud

What looks repetitive on the page lands as rhythm in a hall. Filipino oratory leans on it hard, and audiences expect it.

Para sa bata. Para sa magulang. Para sa bayan.

Hindi ito usapin ng pera. Hindi ito usapin ng panahon. Usapin ito ng dangal.

Diin — stress and emphasis

Diin does aloud what italics do on the page. In Tagalog it usually falls on the fronted element — which is another reason the ay-inversion of Lesson 3 is so common in speeches: it puts the emphasised phrase first, where the stress naturally lands.

Po and ho throughout

A talumpati before elders, officials, or a mixed public audience carries po consistently. Dropping it partway through is jarring — the register must hold from the first line to the last.

6. A Short Model Speech

"Magandang umaga po sa inyong lahat. Sa ating panauhing pandangal, sa mga guro, at sa mga kapwa ko mag-aaral — isang mapagpalang umaga.

Nais ko pong ibahagi ang isang simpleng paniniwala: ang edukasyon ay hindi pribilehiyo, kundi karapatan.

Totoo nga na mahirap ang landas na ito. Ngunit hindi ba't tayo rin ang makikinabang kung ito ay ating ipaglalaban? Para sa bata. Para sa magulang. Para sa bayan.

Bilang pangwakas — huwag po nating kalimutan: ang hindi lumingon sa pinanggalingan ay hindi makararating sa paroroonan. Maraming salamat po at magandang umaga."

Every device in that speech came from an earlier lesson: the ceremonial address, the ay-inversion of the thesis, the concede-then-refute of Lesson 9, a rhetorical question, three-part parallelism, a salawikain from Lesson 13, and the closing signal.

Practice — Speaking Formally

Exercise 1: Name the part

Answer pambungad, katawan, or pangwakas:

  • "Sa ating panauhing pandangal…" → _____ (Answer: pambungad)
  • "Bilang pangwakas…" → _____ (Answer: pangwakas)
  • The two or three main points with their support → _____ (Answer: katawan)

Exercise 2: Complete the set phrase

  • Sa _____ ng aming samahan… (on behalf of) → (Answer: ngalan)
  • Nais ko pong _____ ang mga nag-anyaya. (thank) → (Answer: pasalamatan)
  • _____ pangwakas… (in closing) → (Answer: bilang)
  • Kung inyong _____… (if you will permit me) → (Answer: mamarapatin)

Exercise 3: Oratory vocabulary

  • speech / oration → _____ (Answer: talumpati)
  • orator → _____ (Answer: mananalumpati)
  • guest of honour → _____ (Answer: panauhing pandangal)
  • emphasis / stress → _____ (Answer: diin)

Exercise 4: Which occasion?

Answer parangal, pagtatapos, or pamamaalam:

  • A graduation address → _____ (Answer: pagtatapos)
  • A tribute to someone's service → _____ (Answer: parangal)
  • A restrained farewell → _____ (Answer: pamamaalam)

Exercise 5: Delivery judgement

Answer tama or mali:

  • Dropping "po" halfway through a formal speech → _____ (Answer: mali)
  • Using three-part parallelism in the body → _____ (Answer: tama)
  • Ending without any closing signal → _____ (Answer: mali)

Where This Takes You

You can now stand up and speak Tagalog formally, in a shape a Filipino audience recognises. One lesson remains — the capstone. It steps back from the language to ask what it is: Filipino or Tagalog, whose it is, how it is changing, and how you keep yours alive once this course ends.
