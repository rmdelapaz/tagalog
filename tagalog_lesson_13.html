Mga Pananong - Question Words

The ten little words that let you ask about anything

You've been meeting question words one at a time — ano in Lesson 1, magkano at the market, saan for directions. This lesson gathers them all in one place so you can ask about who, what, where, when, why, how, which, and how much/many with confidence. Master these ten and you can keep almost any conversation moving.

1. The Ten Question Words

TagalogEnglishExample
anowhatAno ito? (What is this?)
sinowhoSino ka? (Who are you?)
saanwhereSaan ka pupunta? (Where are you going?)
kailanwhenKailan ang klase? (When is the class?)
bakitwhyBakit ka umiyak? (Why did you cry?)
paanohow (in what way)Paano ito gawin? (How is this done?)
alinwhichAlin ang gusto mo? (Which do you want?)
ilanhow manyIlan kayo? (How many are you?)
magkanohow much (price)Magkano ito? (How much is this?)
kaninowhose / to whomKanino ito? (Whose is this?)
Two "hows": use paano for in what way ("How do I get there?") and kumusta for how are you / how is it ("Kumusta ka?"). Don't mix them up.

2. The Golden Rule: Question Word First

Unlike English, which sometimes buries the question word, Tagalog almost always puts it right at the front, usually followed by the linker ang or a pronoun:

  • Ano ang pangalan mo? (What is your name?)
  • Saan ka nakatira? (Where do you live?)
  • Sino ang kasama mo? (Who is with you?)
  • Kailan ka darating? (When will you arrive?)

Notice the short pronouns ka, mo slide in right after the question word — a rhythm you'll quickly feel.

3. Answering the Questions

Answers usually mirror the question. Here's each word with a natural reply:

QuestionAnswer
Ano ang trabaho mo? (What's your job?)Guro ako. (I'm a teacher.)
Sino siya? (Who is she?)Kapatid ko siya. (She's my sibling.)
Saan ang banyo? (Where's the bathroom?)Nasa kanan. (On the right.)
Kailan ang kaarawan mo? (When's your birthday?)Sa Enero. (In January.)
Bakit? (Why?)Kasi pagod ako. (Because I'm tired.)
Ilan ang gusto mo? (How many do you want?)Tatlo, salamat. (Three, thanks.)
Magkano? (How much?)Singkwenta pesos. (Fifty pesos.)
Reason answers: reply to bakit with kasi or dahil (both mean "because"). Kasi is the everyday, conversational choice.

4. ano vs. alin, ilan vs. magkano

A few pairs trip people up. Keep these straight:

  • ano = what (open) · alin = which (choosing from options). Ano ang gusto mo? (What do you want?) vs. Alin — tsaa o kape? (Which — tea or coffee?)
  • ilan = how many (counting) · magkano = how much (price). Ilan ang mansanas? (How many apples?) vs. Magkano ang mansanas? (How much are the apples?)

5. Softening & Politeness

Add po (Lesson 1) to be respectful, and ba (Lesson 10) is optional with question words since the word already signals a question:

  • Saan po ang palengke? (Where is the market, politely?)
  • Magkano po ito? (How much is this, politely?)
  • Ano po ang pangalan ninyo? (What is your name, respectfully?)

Practice — Ask Away

Exercise 1: Choose the question word

Fill the blank with the right word (ano, sino, saan, kailan, bakit):

  • "What is this?" → "_____ ito?" (Answer: ano)
  • "Who are you?" → "_____ ka?" (Answer: sino)
  • "Where is the market?" → "_____ ang palengke?" (Answer: saan)
  • "When is the class?" → "_____ ang klase?" (Answer: kailan)
  • "Why are you tired?" → "_____ ka pagod?" (Answer: bakit)

Exercise 2: how many, how much, or which?

Fill with ilan, magkano, or alin:

  • "How much is this?" → "_____ ito?" (Answer: magkano)
  • "How many are you?" → "_____ kayo?" (Answer: ilan)
  • "Which do you want?" → "_____ ang gusto mo?" (Answer: alin)

Exercise 3: Match the answer

Which question word does this answer fit? (write ano, sino, saan, magkano, or bakit)

  • Answer: "Guro ako." (I'm a teacher.) → question word: _____ (Answer: ano)
  • Answer: "Kasi pagod ako." (Because I'm tired.) → _____ (Answer: bakit)
  • Answer: "Nasa kanan." (On the right.) → _____ (Answer: saan)

Why This Lesson Matters

Questions are how a conversation keeps going and how a beginner learns fastest — every question you ask invites new language back. With these ten words you can find your way, shop, meet people, and clear up anything you don't understand.
