Pakikisalamuha — Social & Reciprocal Verbs

Two ways verbs reach toward other people: joining in (maki-) and doing things to each other (mag-…-an)

Filipino culture runs on togetherness — pakikisama (getting along), sharing meals, doing things as a group. Tagalog has grammar built for exactly this, two affix patterns that turn an action outward toward other people:

  • maki- — join in someone else's action, or ask to take part: makikain (join the meal), makisakay (hitch a ride), makiusap (make a request).
  • mag-…-an — do something to each other, mutually: mag-usap (talk with each other), magkita (meet), magtulungan (help one another).

Both are actor focus — the participants wear ang — so they slot right into the family you started in Lesson 1.

1. maki- : Joining In

maki- means you take part in an action that is really someone else's — you join their meal, their ride, their group — or you approach someone with a request. The joiner is the actor, in the ang spotlight; the person or group you join takes sa (or kay before a name).

The pattern: maki- → naki- for started aspects, and ki reduplicates for present/future (nakiki-, makiki-). More social verbs:

  • Nakikain ako sa kapitbahay. — I ate over at the neighbor's.
  • Nakisakay si Lito kay Ben. — Lito hitched a ride with Ben.
  • Nakiusap ako sa boss na umuwi nang maaga. — I asked the boss (as a favor) to go home early.
The polite cousin: paki-. Remember paki- from the beginner course (pakibuksan = "please open")? It's maki-'s close relative — both come from the idea of involving another person. maki- = "let me take part"; paki- = "please do this for me." Same social root, two everyday tools.

2. mag-…-an : Doing It to Each Other

The reciprocal says two (or more) people do the action to one another — mutually. It uses mag- on the front, very often with -an on the end, and it needs a plural actor in focus (kami, sila, tayo, or sina X at Y):

AspectForm (usap)Meaning
Infinitivemag-usapto talk with each other
Completed (past)nag-usaptalked with each other
Incompleted (present)nag-uusapare talking with each other
Contemplated (future)mag-uusapwill talk with each other

It's the ordinary mag- aspect machine (mag- → nag-, first syllable reduplicating). More reciprocals:

  • Nagkita sina Ana at Ben kahapon. — Ana and Ben met yesterday.
  • Nagtutulungan ang mga kapitbahay. — The neighbors help one another.

3. Reciprocal vs. One-Directional

The reciprocal's whole job is the "each other" arrow going both ways. Contrast it with an ordinary one-directional verb, where the action flows one way only:

One-directionalReciprocal (mag-…-an)
Kinausap ko si Ben. — I talked to Ben. (I did it; he listened)Nag-usap kami ni Ben. — Ben and I talked. (both of us, mutually)
Tinulungan niya ako. — She helped me. (one way)Nagtulungan kami. — We helped each other. (both ways)
Watch the actor. Because the action is mutual, a reciprocal takes a plural or joined subject: kami, sila, tayo, or sina Ana at Ben. You'll also hear the … ni / kina pattern to name the other party: Nag-usap ako ni Ben ("Ben and I talked"). A single lone subject can't act reciprocally — it takes two.

4. Both Are Actor Focus

Step back and the tidy part appears: maki- and mag-…-an are both actor-focus patterns — the participant(s) wear ang, just like -um- and mag- back in Lesson 1. What's new is only the social shape of the action:

PatternWho's in angSocial meaning
maki-the joiner (one person)take part in another's action / request
mag-…-anthe mutual actors (plural)do it to each other
-um- / mag-the plain doerordinary action (Lesson 1)

So there's nothing to un-learn: the marker rules are identical. You're just choosing the affix whose social angle matches what you mean — solo, joining, or mutual.

5. Everyday Social Verbs

Keep these ready — they come up in real conversation constantly:

Pakikisama is a value, not just a verb. Makisama ("get along, go with the group") names one of the most prized Filipino social skills — being easy to be with, not causing friction. When someone is praised as marunong makisama ("knows how to get along"), it's a real compliment about character, not merely grammar.

Exercise 1: Conjugate maki-

Give the requested aspect:

  • sama → get along, completed: _____ (Answer: nakisama)
  • usap → make a request, completed: _____ (Answer: nakiusap)
  • sakay → ride along, incompleted (present): _____ (Answer: nakikisakay)
  • sama → get along, contemplated (future): _____ (Answer: makikisama)

Exercise 2: Conjugate the reciprocal

Give the requested aspect:

  • usap → talk with each other, completed: _____ (Answer: nag-usap)
  • kita → meet each other, completed: _____ (Answer: nagkita)
  • tulong → help each other (magtulungan), incompleted (present): _____ (Answer: nagtutulungan)
  • kita → meet each other, contemplated (future): _____ (Answer: magkikita)

Exercise 3: Joining in or each other?

Answer join (maki-) or reciprocal (each other):

  • Nakikain ako sa kapitbahay. → _____ (Answer: join|joining|maki)
  • Nag-usap sina Ana at Ben. → _____ (Answer: reciprocal|each other)
  • Nakisakay si Lito kay Ben. → _____ (Answer: join|joining|maki)

Exercise 4: Supply the marker

Fill in ang or sa:

  • Nakiusap ako _____ boss. → (Answer: sa)
  • Nag-away _____ magkapatid. (the two siblings = the mutual actors) → (Answer: ang)
  • Nakikain ang bata _____ kanila. → (Answer: sa)

Exercise 5: Say it with the right verb

Give the verb (completed aspect) for each situation:

  • You joined your neighbors for dinner. → _____ ako sa kapitbahay. (Answer: nakikain)
  • You and your friend talked things over. → _____ kami. (Answer: nag-usap)
  • You pleaded with the teacher (asked a favor). → _____ ako sa guro. (Answer: nakiusap)

Where This Takes You

You can now aim verbs at other people two ways: maki- to join in (one joiner in ang, the group in sa) and mag-…-an to do things to each other (plural actors in ang). Both stay inside the actor-focus family — same markers, new social angle — and one of them, makisama, names a whole Filipino value. That closes out the B1 verb-affix arc (locative, benefactive, causative, ability, social).
