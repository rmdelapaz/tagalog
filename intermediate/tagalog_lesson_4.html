Mga Pandiwang Sanhi — Causative Verbs (magpa- / ipa-)

Not doing it yourself — having someone else do it for you

So far every verb has had you doing the action. But real life is full of actions you arrange rather than perform: you get a haircut, you have the house repaired, you have someone summoned. In Tagalog this "have it done" idea has its own affixes — the causative prefixes magpa- and ipa-.

Causatives add a new character to the scene: the causer (the one who wants it done) and the causee (the one who actually does it). And because Tagalog is a focus language, there are two ways to build it depending on who's in the spotlight — the causer (magpa-) or the thing being done (ipa-).

1. The Cast: Causer, Causee, Action

Every causative sentence has up to three players. Keep them straight and the affixes fall into place:

  • Causer — the one who wants the action done (often "I").
  • Causee — the one who actually does it (the cook, the barber, the worker). Marked with sa or ng.
  • Patient / thing — what the action is done to (the food, the hair, the house).

The prefix pa- is the causative core — it means "cause/let to be done." magpa- and ipa- are just pa- wrapped for two different focuses.

2. magpa- : the Causer in Focus

magpa- is an actor-focus causative — but the "actor" it spotlights is the causer, the one who had it done. So the causer wears ang (or is an ang-pronoun like ako):

  • Nagpaluto ako ng adobo sa kusinero. — I had the cook make adobo. causer-focus
  • Nagpagupit si Ben sa barbero. — Ben got a haircut (had the barber cut it).

The causer (ako, si Ben) is in focus; the causee takes sa; the thing takes ng. Literally: "I had-cook adobo at/with the cook."

Aspect works with the usual mag- pattern — the m becomes n for started aspects, and the syllable after pa… actually reduplicates pa itself:

AspectPrefixExample (luto)
Infinitive / commandmagpa-magpaluto
Completed (past)nagpa-nagpaluto
Incompleted (present)nagpapa-nagpapaluto
Contemplated (future)magpapa-magpapaluto

3. ipa- : the Thing Done in Focus

ipa- is the object/patient-focus causative: it puts ang on the thing that gets done. Now the causer drops to ng (or a ng-pronoun like ko), and the causee still takes sa:

  • Ipinaluto ko ang manok kay Ana. — I had the chicken cooked by Ana. patient-focus
  • Ipinagawa namin ang bahay. — We had the house built/repaired.

Here the thing (ang manok, ang bahay) is in the spotlight — natural when the thing is specific and it's what you're talking about. Compare with magpa-, where you the causer were the news.

Aspect uses the object-focus -in- machinery, landing right after the i:

AspectPrefixExample (luto)
Infinitive / commandipa-ipaluto
Completed (past)ipina-ipinaluto
Incompleted (present)ipinapa-ipinapaluto
Contemplated (future)ipapa-ipapaluto

4. magpa- vs. ipa-: Same Event, Which Spotlight?

It's the focus system again — one causative event, framed by whichever role is the news. Pick magpa- when the person who arranged it is the topic; pick ipa- when the thing done is the topic:

CausativeSpotlight (ang)Example
magpa-the causerNagpaluto ako ng manok sa kusinero.
ipa-the thing doneIpinaluto ko ang manok sa kusinero.
How to remember which. magpa- starts like mag- (actor focus) — so it spotlights the doer-of-the-arranging, the causer. ipa- starts like the object-marking i- family — so it spotlights the thing. Match your choice to the answer you'd give: "Sino ang nagpaluto?" (Who had it cooked?) → magpa-; "Ano ang ipinaluto?" (What was had-cooked?) → ipa-.

5. Marking the Causee: sa / ng

The causee — whoever actually performs the action — is normally marked with sa (or kay before a personal name). You can often leave the causee out entirely when it's obvious or unimportant:

  • Nagpagupit ako sa barbero. — I got a haircut from the barber. (causee named)
  • Nagpagupit ako. — I got a haircut. (causee dropped — a barber is implied)
  • Ipinaayos ko ang kotse sa mekaniko. — I had the car fixed by the mechanic.
Everyday causatives you'll hear constantly. Filipinos use causatives for all the services of daily life:
  • nagpagupit — got a haircut
  • nagpagawa — had something made/built/repaired
  • nagpaluto — had food cooked
  • nagpahinga — rested (had oneself rest — pa- can turn inward, too)
  • ipinatawag — had someone summoned / sent for someone
  • ipinaayos / ipinagawa — had something fixed / made

6. Putting It in a Sentence

A few fuller examples to hear the rhythm — notice the causer, causee, and thing each in place:

  • Nagpapaluto si Nanay ng hapunan sa kasambahay tuwing Linggo. — Mom has the helper cook dinner every Sunday.
  • Ipinatawag ng boss si Ana. — The boss sent for Ana. (Ana = the one summoned, in focus)
  • Magpapagawa kami ng bagong pinto sa karpintero. — We'll have the carpenter make a new door.
  • Ipinaluto ko ang isda kagabi. — I had the fish cooked last night.

Practice — Work the Causatives

Exercise 1: Conjugate the causative

Give the requested aspect:

  • magpaluto, completed (past): _____ (Answer: nagpaluto)
  • magpaluto, incompleted (present): _____ (Answer: nagpapaluto)
  • ipaluto, completed (past): _____ (Answer: ipinaluto)
  • ipaluto, contemplated (future): _____ (Answer: ipapaluto)

Exercise 2: magpa- or ipa-?

Which causative matches the spotlight — answer magpa or ipa:

  • _____ ako ng adobo sa kusinero. (causer "ako" in focus) → (Answer: magpa|nagpa)
  • _____ ko ang manok sa kusinero. (thing "ang manok" in focus) → (Answer: ipa|ipina)
  • _____ kami ng bahay sa karpintero. (causer "kami" in focus) → (Answer: magpa|nagpa)

Exercise 3: Mark the causee

Fill in the marker for the causee (the one who does the work). Use sa or kay:

  • Nagpaluto ako ng pansit _____ kusinero. → (Answer: sa)
  • Ipinaayos niya ang relo _____ Mang Tonyo. (a person's name) → (Answer: kay)
  • Nagpagupit si Ana _____ bagong barbero. → (Answer: sa)

Exercise 4: Spot the causer's marker

In each sentence, is the causer in focus (ang-word) or non-focus (ng-word)? Answer ang or ng:

  • Nagpaluto ako ng manok. → _____ (Answer: ang)
  • Ipinaluto ko ang manok. → _____ (Answer: ng)
  • Nagpapagawa si Nanay ng damit. → _____ (Answer: ang)

Exercise 5: Say it with a causative

Give the everyday causative verb (completed aspect) for each:

  • You got a haircut. → _____ ako. (Answer: nagpagupit)
  • The boss sent for Ana. → _____ ng boss si Ana. (Answer: ipinatawag)
  • We had a new house built. → _____ kami ng bagong bahay. (Answer: nagpagawa)

Where This Takes You

Causatives let you talk about the huge slice of life you arrange rather than do yourself. Two spotlights, one idea: magpa- keeps the causer in focus ("I had it done"); ipa- keeps the thing done in focus ("the chicken was had-cooked"), with the causee tucked into a sa-phrase.
