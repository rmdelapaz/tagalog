Mga Kondisyonal — Conditionals

kung, kapag, and sakali: talking about if, when, and in case

Lesson 8 gave you the glue for joining ideas. One kind of link is important enough to deserve its own lesson: the conditional — the "if this, then that" that lets you talk about possibilities, plans, and rules. Tagalog splits this job across three words, and choosing the right one is mostly about how certain the condition is.

Get the kung / kapag / sakali distinction and you can promise, warn, plan, and speculate — the backbone of grown-up conversation.

1. Three Ways to Say "If / When"

They overlap in English translation but differ in feel:

  • kung — if / whether: the condition is uncertain or hypothetical. (Kung uulan… = If it rains…)
  • kapag (short: pag) — when / whenever: the condition is expected, real, or habitual. (Kapag umuulan… = When[ever] it rains…)
  • sakali (kung sakali, sakaling) — in case / should it happen: a contingency you're preparing for. (Sakaling umulan… = In case it rains…)

A conditional has two parts: the condition (the "if" clause) and the result (what follows). Either can come first — the connector marks the condition clause.

2. kung — If / Whether (uncertain)

Use kung when you're not sure the condition will happen — it's hypothetical or open:

  • Kung uulan bukas, hindi tayo aalis. — If it rains tomorrow, we won't go.
  • Kung may pera ako, bibili ako ng bagong telepono. — If I had money, I'd buy a new phone.
  • Kung gusto mo, samahan kita. — If you want, I'll go with you.
kung also means "whether." Beyond conditions, kung introduces embedded (indirect) questions: Hindi ko alam kung darating siya. ("I don't know whether/if he'll come.") Tanungin mo kung bukas ba sila. ("Ask if they're open.") Same word, closely related idea of an open possibility.

3. kapag — When / Whenever (real, habitual)

Use kapag when the condition is expected to happen, is a general truth, or repeats. It's closer to English "when(ever)" than "if":

  • Kapag umuulan, nagbabaha sa amin. — When(ever) it rains, it floods in our area. (a regular truth)
  • Kapag natapos ako, tatawag ako sa iyo. — When I'm done, I'll call you. (expected)
  • Pag uwi ko, magluluto ako. — When I get home, I'll cook. (pag = casual short form)

Because kapag conditions are treated as real/recurring, it pairs naturally with habitual imperfective verbs (umuulan, nagbabaha) — a direct callback to the habitual reading you learned in Lesson 7.

When either works. In casual speech Filipinos often blur the two, and both can be fine for a plain future condition. But the instinct holds: kapag leans "this will/does happen," kung leans "this might happen." Reach for kapag with sunrise-certain events (Kapag umaga…) and kung with genuine maybes.

5. sakali — In Case / Should It Happen

sakali (usually sakaling or kung sakali) marks a just-in-case contingency — something you're preparing for even though it probably won't happen:

  • Sakaling umulan, may dala akong payong. — In case it rains, I'm carrying an umbrella.
  • Kung sakaling mahuli ako, mauna na kayo. — Should I be late, go ahead without me.
  • Magtabi ka ng pera para sa sakaling emergency. — Set aside money for a possible emergency.

Think of sakali as the "contingency plan" connector: it flags that you've thought ahead about a possibility, however remote.

6. Verb Aspect in Conditionals

Which aspect goes in each clause? A reliable default for future conditions:

ClauseTypical aspectExample
Condition (kung/kapag/sakali)contemplated (future) or imperfective (habitual)Kung uulan… / Kapag umuulan…
Resultcontemplated (future) or a command…hindi tayo aalis. / …magdala ka ng payong.
Rule of thumb. For "if X happens, Y will happen," put both verbs in the contemplated (future): Kung aalis ka, sasama ako. For a habit ("whenever X, Y"), use the imperfective in both: Kapag umuulan, nagbabaha. Match the aspect to whether you mean a one-time future or a repeating pattern — exactly the choice from Lesson 7.

Exercise 1: kung, kapag, or sakali?

Fill the blank with the connector for the meaning in parentheses:

  • _____ Pasko, masaya ang lahat. (when — it will come) → (Answer: kapag|pag)
  • _____ umulan, may dala akong payong. (in case) → (Answer: sakali|sakaling|kung sakali)
  • _____ gutom ka na, kumain ka na. (whenever — real) → (Answer: kapag|pag)

  • If I somehow finish early (I might not). → _____ (Answer: kung)
  • When payday comes (it will). → _____ (Answer: kapag|pag)

Exercise 3: "whether" kung

  • Tanungin mo _____ bukas ba sila. (if / whether) → (Answer: kung)

Exercise 4: Aspect in the clause

  • Kung (ulan) _____ , hindi tayo aalis. — root: ulan (umulan) → (Answer: uulan)

Where This Takes You

Conditionals turn on certainty: kung for the uncertain "if" (and "whether"), kapag for the expected or habitual "when," and sakali for a "just in case" contingency — with the clause aspect (contemplated for one-off futures, imperfective for habits) picked exactly as in Lesson 7.
