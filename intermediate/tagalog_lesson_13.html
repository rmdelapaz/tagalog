Rehistro at Pormalidad — Register & Formality

Welcome to B2. The same idea in Tagalog can sound scholarly, standard, casual, or Taglish — learn to read and control the dial

You reach B2 already able to say most things. The upper-intermediate skill is saying them the right way for the situation — formally in a letter, warmly with family, breezily with friends, half-English at the office. That's register. This lesson maps the Tagalog register spectrum and gives you the tools to move along it deliberately.

1. The Register Spectrum

Think of Tagalog as sliding along a dial from deepest/most literary to most mixed-with-English:

None is "better." A wedding speech wants the formal end; a group chat wants the casual/Taglish end. Mismatch the register and you sound stiff — or sloppy — for the moment.

3. Politeness Markers

Register isn't only vocabulary — it's how you mark respect. The core tools:

  • po / opo — the all-purpose respect particles. Salamat po. / Opo (respectful yes). (ho / oho are softer, less formal variants.)
  • kayo / sila for a single person — using the plural "you" (kayo) or even "they" (sila) to address one elder or superior shows deference.
  • Titles — Ginoo (Mr.), Binibini/Bb. (Miss), Ginang/Gng. (Mrs.), and respectful kin terms (Tita, Tito, Kuya, Ate) even for non-relatives.
  • Casual: Kumusta ka? → Respectful: Kumusta po kayo? — How are you?
  • Casual: Anong pangalan mo? → Respectful: Ano po ang pangalan ninyo?

4. Taglish: When & Why

Taglish — fluidly mixing Tagalog and English — isn't broken language; it's the natural register of urban, educated, and professional Filipino life. English words slot into Tagalog grammar, often taking Tagalog affixes:

  • Nag-meeting kami kanina. — We had a meeting earlier. (meeting + mag-)
  • Pwede mo bang i-send ang file? — Can you send the file? (send + i-)
  • Na-stress ako sa trabaho. — I got stressed at work. (stress + ma-)
Taglish is a skill, not a failure. Knowing when to code-switch is part of sounding native. Technical, corporate, and pop-culture topics lean heavily Taglish; heartfelt, rural, ceremonial, or official contexts lean pure Tagalog. Reading the room and shifting is exactly the B2 competence.

  • kwarto → _____ (Answer: casual)
  • ngunit → _____ (Answer: formal)
  • eskwelahan → _____ (Answer: casual)

Exercise 2: Give the formal equivalent

Provide the formal/deep word:

  • salita (language) → _____ (Answer: wika)
  • opisina (office) → _____ (Answer: tanggapan)
  • pero (but) → _____ (Answer: ngunit|subalit)

Exercise 3: Make it respectful

Fill in the politeness particle:

  • Salamat _____. (respect particle) → (Answer: po)
  • Kumusta _____ kayo? (respect particle) → (Answer: po)
  • _____ (respectful "yes") → (Answer: opo)

Exercise 4: Spot the Taglish affix

Give the Tagalog affix wrapping the English root (e.g., nag-, i-, na-):

  • ___-meeting kami. (actor-focus, completed) → (Answer: nag)
  • Pwede mo bang ___-send? (object-focus request) → (Answer: i)
  • ___-stress ako. (accidental/ma-, completed) → (Answer: na)

Exercise 5: Which register fits?

Answer formal or Taglish:

  • A eulogy at a memorial → _____ (Answer: formal)
  • A project chat with officemates → _____ (Answer: Taglish|taglish)

Where This Takes You

Register is the B2 mindset: the same meaning dressed for its occasion. You can now slide from deep to formal to casual to Taglish, swap word pairs (wika/salita), mark respect with po/kayo, and code-switch on purpose.
