Mga Opinyon, Damdamin at Dahilan — Opinions, Feelings & Reasons

Say what you think, how you feel, and why — the heart of real conversation

You now have the grammar to build complex sentences. This lesson gives you the moves that make conversation personal: opening an opinion, naming a feeling, and backing it up with a reason. These are the phrases Filipinos actually use to agree, disagree, worry, hope, and explain — and they recycle everything you've learned (linkers, connectors, aspect).

1. Opening an Opinion

Lead with one of these set phrases, then say your view. They all mean roughly "I think / in my opinion":

OpenerLiteralUse
Sa palagay ko…in my estimationI think… (very common)
Sa tingin ko…in my viewI think / it seems to me…
Para sa akin…for me…as I see it / for my part
Sa pakiramdam ko…in my feelingI feel that…
  • Sa palagay ko, tama ang desisyon niya. — I think her decision is right.
  • Sa tingin ko, masyadong mahal ito. — I think this is too expensive.
  • Para sa akin, mas maganda ang una. — For me, the first one is nicer.

2. Believing, Thinking & "It Seems"

Beyond the openers, a few verbs and hedges express degrees of conviction:

ExpressionMeaning
Naniniwala ako na…I believe that…
Sigurado ako na…I'm sure that…
Mukhang… / Parang…It seems / it looks like… (a hedge)
Siguro / Baka…Maybe / perhaps (uncertain)
  • Naniniwala ako na magiging maayos ang lahat. — I believe everything will be fine.
  • Mukhang uulan mamaya. — It looks like it'll rain later.
  • Baka hindi siya sasama. — Maybe she won't come along.
Softening is polite. Filipinos often cushion opinions with hedges like parang, siguro, and sa palagay ko rather than stating things bluntly — it keeps harmony (part of that pakikisama value from Lesson 6). "Parang mali yata…" ("It seems maybe wrong…") is gentler than a flat "That's wrong."

3. Naming Feelings

Most core emotions are ma- adjectives or na- state verbs. Pair them with ako and, if you like, a reason:

TagalogFeeling
masayahappy / glad
malungkotsad
natutuwadelighted / pleased
nababahala / nag-aalalaworried / anxious
galit / nagagalitangry
takot / natatakotafraid
excited / sabikexcited / eager
  • Masaya ako dahil umuwi ka. — I'm happy because you came home.
  • Nag-aalala ako tungkol sa exam. — I'm worried about the exam.
  • Natutuwa ako sa balita! — I'm delighted at the news!

4. Giving the Reason

An opinion or feeling lands harder with a why. Recycle the cause connectors from Lesson 8 — kasi / dahil (because) and kaya (so):

  • Sa palagay ko masarap ito dahil sariwa ang sangkap. — I think this is tasty because the ingredients are fresh.
  • Hindi ako sasama kasi may sakit ako. — I won't come because I'm sick.
  • Mahal ang bilihin ngayon, kaya nagtitipid kami. — Goods are expensive now, so we're economizing.

That's the whole recipe: [opinion/feeling] + [reason with kasi/dahil]. Simple, and instantly more persuasive.

5. Agreeing & Disagreeing

Respond to someone else's view with these:

AgreeDisagree (gently)
Sang-ayon ako. — I agree.Hindi ako sang-ayon. — I disagree.
Tama ka. — You're right.Sa tingin ko naman… — I, on the other hand, think…
Totoo nga. — That's true.Hindi ako sigurado diyan. — I'm not sure about that.
The polite pivot: naman. To disagree without confrontation, Filipinos often use naman ("on the other hand") plus a soft opener: "Sa tingin ko naman, mas mabuti ang isa." It signals a different view while keeping things friendly — much softer than a blunt "Mali ka" ("You're wrong").

6. Putting It Together

Opener + claim + reason + (a soft agree/disagree) — that's a complete, natural opinion turn:

"I think the first plan is better because it's cheaper and faster. But I also agree that we should be careful."

Practice — Say What You Think

Exercise 1: Open the opinion

Fill in the opener for the meaning:

  • Sa _____ ko, tama siya. (I think — "estimation") → (Answer: palagay)
  • Sa _____ ko, masyadong mahal. (I think — "view") → (Answer: tingin)
  • _____ sa akin, mas maganda ang una. (for me) → (Answer: para)

Exercise 2: Name the feeling

Give the Tagalog feeling word:

  • happy → _____ (Answer: masaya)
  • sad → _____ (Answer: malungkot)
  • worried → _____ (Answer: nag-aalala|nababahala)

Exercise 3: Add the reason

Fill in dahil/kasi (because) or kaya (so):

  • Masaya ako _____ umuwi ka. (because) → (Answer: dahil|kasi)
  • May sakit ako, _____ hindi ako sasama. (so) → (Answer: kaya)
  • Hindi ako pumunta _____ umuulan. (because) → (Answer: dahil|kasi)

Exercise 4: Agree or disagree?

Answer agree or disagree:

  • Sang-ayon ako. → _____ (Answer: agree)
  • Hindi ako sang-ayon. → _____ (Answer: disagree)
  • Tama ka. → _____ (Answer: agree)

Exercise 5: The hedge

Fill in mukhang/parang (it seems) or baka/siguro (maybe):

  • _____ uulan mamaya. (it looks like) → (Answer: mukhang|parang)
  • _____ hindi siya darating. (maybe) → (Answer: baka|siguro)

Where This Takes You

You can now run a full opinion turn: open with sa palagay/tingin ko, name a feeling with a ma- word, justify it with kasi/dahil, and respond with sang-ayon ako or the gentle naman pivot — all cushioned with hedges like parang and siguro to keep harmony. It's the social payoff of everything so far. The final B1 lesson channels it all into a single skill: telling stories — narrating experiences with time and sequence.
