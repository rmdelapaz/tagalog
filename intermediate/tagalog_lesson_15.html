Di-tuwirang Pananalita — Reported Speech

Relaying what people said: sabi niya na…, the reportative daw, and tinanong kung…

Conversation is full of relaying: "She said she'll come." "He asked if you're free." "They say it's closed." That's reported (indirect) speech, and Tagalog handles it with a few reliable tools — the quotative sabi, the little reportative particle daw/raw, and (a callback to Lesson 9) kung for reported questions. Best of all, Tagalog has no awkward tense-backshift like English — the aspect stays put.

1. Direct vs. Reported

Direct speech quotes the exact words; reported speech folds them into your sentence, shifting the pronouns to your point of view:

  • Direct: Sabi ni Ana, "Pupunta ako." — Ana said, "I'll go."
  • Reported: Sabi ni Ana na pupunta siya. — Ana said (that) she'll go.

Notice two shifts: the quote-marks give way to the linker na, and ako ("I", Ana's word) becomes siya ("she", your word). The verb pupunta — its aspect — does not change.

2. sabi / sinabi: "He Said (That)…"

The everyday quotative is sabi (+ ni/nila for the speaker). More formally you'll see sinabi (the object-focus verb "said"):

FrameExample
Sabi ni [speaker] na…Sabi ni Ben na uuwi siya. — Ben said he'll go home.
Sabi niya na…Sabi niya na busy siya. — She said she's busy.
Sinabi ni [speaker] na…Sinabi ng guro na may pasok bukas. — The teacher said there's class tomorrow.
No backshift — keep the aspect. English shifts "will → would," "is → was" in reported speech. Tagalog doesn't. If the original was pupunta (contemplated), it stays pupunta; if it was kumakain (imperfective), it stays. You only change the pronouns to match who's speaking now.

3. The Reportative daw / raw

The single most Filipino way to report is the enclitic particle daw (after a consonant) / raw (after a vowel) — "reportedly / they say / (someone) said." It slips into the sentence and instantly marks the info as second-hand:

  • Aalis daw sila bukas. — They said they're leaving tomorrow. / They'll reportedly leave tomorrow.
  • Masaya raw ang pelikula. — The movie's good, they say.
  • Wala raw pasok kasi may bagyo. — No class, reportedly, because of the storm.
daw/raw shields you from the claim. Using it says "this isn't my own knowledge — I'm passing it on." It's everywhere in Filipino gossip, news relay, and everyday hedging. Position: it sits right after the first word/phrase, like other enclitics (na, pa, ba).

4. Reporting Questions with kung

To report a question, use a "tanong" verb (nagtanong, tinanong) plus kung ("whether/if") — exactly the embedded-question kung from Lesson 9:

  • Tinanong niya kung pupunta ako. — She asked whether I'd come.
  • Nagtanong sila kung saan ang tindahan. — They asked where the store is.
  • Tinanong ko kung okay lang siya. — I asked if she was okay.

Yes/no questions take kung; "wh-" questions keep their question word (saan, ano, kailan) after the reporting verb: Tinanong niya kung ano ang gusto ko.

5. Ayon kay: "According To"

To attribute information to a source — common in news and careful speech — use ayon kay (+ name) / ayon sa (+ common noun):

  • Ayon kay Ana, sarado ang tindahan. — According to Ana, the store is closed.
  • Ayon sa balita, tataas ang presyo. — According to the news, prices will rise.

Practice — Report What Was Said

Exercise 1: daw or raw?

Choose the correct form (raw after a vowel, daw after a consonant):

  • Masaya _____ ang pelikula. (after vowel "masaya") → (Answer: raw)
  • Aalis _____ sila. (after consonant "aalis") → (Answer: daw)
  • Wala _____ pasok. (after vowel "wala") → (Answer: raw)

Exercise 2: Shift the pronoun

Report the quote — give the pronoun that replaces the speaker's "ako":

  • Sabi ni Ben, "Uuwi ako." → Sabi ni Ben na uuwi _____. (Answer: siya)
  • Sabi nila, "Sasama kami." → Sabi nila na sasama _____. (Answer: sila)

Exercise 3: Report the question

Fill in the word that introduces a reported yes/no question:

  • Tinanong niya _____ pupunta ako. (whether) → (Answer: kung)
  • Tinanong ko _____ okay lang siya. (if) → (Answer: kung)

Exercise 4: Keep the aspect (no backshift)

The quote's verb stays the same aspect — supply it:

  • "Kumakain ako." → Sabi niya na _____ siya. (Answer: kumakain)
  • "Aalis ako." → Sabi niya na _____ siya. (Answer: aalis)

Exercise 5: "According to"

Fill in kay (before a name) or sa (before a common noun):

  • Ayon _____ Ana, sarado na. → (Answer: kay)
  • Ayon _____ balita, uulan. → (Answer: sa)

Where This Takes You

You can now relay speech three ways: the quotative sabi/sinabi … na, the reportative particle daw/raw ("they say"), and reported questions with kung (Lesson 9 again) — plus ayon kay/sa to name a source. Remember the two habits: shift the pronouns, keep the aspect (no backshift). Next we push conditionals into the unreal: hypotheticals with sana and counterfactual "if."
