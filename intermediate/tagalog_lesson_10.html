Mga Sugnay na Panuring — Relative Clauses & Rich Description

"The person who…", "the thing that…", and how to say very, too, and kind of

You can already stick a single adjective onto a noun with the linker — magandang bahay (beautiful house). This lesson scales that up two ways. First, you'll attach a whole clause to a noun ("the house that we bought," "the woman who is singing") — a relative clause. Second, you'll turn description up and down with intensifiers — napaka-, sobrang, masyadong, medyo. Together they let you describe with real precision.

1. The na / -ng Linker, Revisited

The engine behind all Tagalog description is the linker na / -ng. It clips a modifier to the thing it describes. The rule from the beginner course still holds:

  • After a word ending in a vowel, -n, or glottal stop → use -ng (attached): magandang bahay.
  • After any other consonant → use na (separate word): mabait na tao.

And the linker is reversible — modifier and noun can swap order: magandang bahay = bahay na maganda. That flexibility is exactly what makes relative clauses possible.

2. Relative Clauses: "the ___ that ___"

Swap the single adjective for a verb phrase and the same linker builds a relative clause — a mini-sentence describing the noun:

  • ang babaeng kumakanta — the woman who is singing
  • ang librong binili ko — the book that I bought
  • ang lalaking nakatira sa tabi namin — the man who lives next to us
  • ang pagkaing niluto ni Nanay — the food that Mom cooked

English uses "who / that / which"; Tagalog just uses the linker na/-ng for all of them. The clause can be as long as you like — everything after the linker describes the noun.

3. The Focus Rule (a Big Callback)

Here's where Lesson 1 pays off. In a Tagalog relative clause, the noun being described must be the focus of the verb inside the clause. So you choose the verb's focus to match the role the noun plays:

The noun is the…Use this focusExample
doeractor focusang lalaking bumili ng kotse — the man who bought a car
thing acted onobject focusang kotseng binili ng lalaki — the car that the man bought
Same scene, two descriptions. "The man bought the car" can describe either noun — but you must switch the verb's focus to point at the one you're describing. Describing the man? Actor focus (bumili). Describing the car? Object focus (binili). This is the exact ang↔ng logic from Lesson 1, now doing descriptive work. You can't relativize a non-focus noun directly — you re-aim the focus instead.

4. Intensifiers: Turning Description Up and Down

Now the dial. These words strengthen or soften an adjective:

WordForceExample
napaka-very / so (strong, prefixed to the root)napakaganda — very beautiful
sobrangextremely / so muchsobrang init — extremely hot
masyadongtoo (often negative — excess)masyadong mahal — too expensive
medyosomewhat / kind of (softens)medyo pagod — kind of tired
  • Napakabait ng guro namin. — Our teacher is so kind.
  • Sobrang saya ko ngayon! — I'm so happy today!
  • Masyadong maalat ang sabaw. — The soup is too salty.
  • Medyo malayo pa ang bahay nila. — Their house is still kind of far.
napaka- vs. sobrang vs. masyadong. napaka- and sobrang are both strong positives — genuine admiration or intensity. masyadong usually carries a note of too much (a complaint): masyadong mainit = "too hot" (uncomfortably). medyo is the gentle dial-down. Choosing among them colors your whole sentence's attitude.

5. Stacking Description

Chain a relative clause and an intensifier and you're painting a full picture:

  • ang napakasarap na pagkaing niluto ni Nanay — the very delicious food that Mom cooked
  • ang batang medyo mahiyahing nakaupo sa likod — the somewhat shy child who is sitting at the back
  • isang sobrang gandang lugar na napuntahan namin — an extremely beautiful place that we got to visit

Every join is the same linker doing its quiet work. Once you hear na/-ng as "the describing glue," rich Tagalog description stops feeling like a wall and starts feeling like Lego.

Practice — Describe with Precision

Exercise 1: na or -ng?

Give the correct linker for each pair:

  • maganda ___ bahay (ends in vowel) → (Answer: -ng|ng)
  • mabait ___ tao (ends in consonant) → (Answer: na)
  • libro ___ binili ko (ends in vowel) → (Answer: -ng|ng)

Exercise 2: Which focus for the relative clause?

Answer AF (actor focus) or OF (object focus):

  • ang lalaking bumili ng kotse (describing the man = doer) → _____ (Answer: AF|af|actor)
  • ang kotseng binili ng lalaki (describing the car = thing) → _____ (Answer: OF|of|object)
  • ang babaeng kumakanta (describing the singer = doer) → _____ (Answer: AF|af|actor)
  • ang pagkaing niluto ni Nanay (describing the food = thing) → _____ (Answer: OF|of|object)

Exercise 3: Choose the intensifier

Fill in napaka-, masyadong, or medyo for the meaning:

  • _____ganda ng tanawin. (very — one word with the root) → (Answer: napaka)
  • _____ mahal ito, hindi ko kaya. (too — a complaint) → (Answer: masyadong)
  • _____ pagod ako, pero okay lang. (kind of — softened) → (Answer: medyo)

Exercise 4: Build the relative clause

Give the linker (na/-ng) that joins the noun to its clause:

  • ang tao ___ tumulong sa akin → (Answer: na)
  • ang mangga ___ kinain mo → (Answer: -ng|ng)

Exercise 5: Strong or soft?

Answer strong (intensifies) or soft (weakens):

  • sobrang saya → _____ (Answer: strong)
  • medyo malamig → _____ (Answer: soft)
  • napakalaki → _____ (Answer: strong)

Where This Takes You

The linker na/-ng is the single most productive tool in Tagalog description: it clips single adjectives and whole relative clauses onto nouns — with the clause verb's focus aimed at the noun you're describing (Lesson 1, again). Add the intensifier dial — napaka-, sobrang, masyadong, medyo — and you control both what and how much. Next, you'll put description to work expressing yourself: opinions, feelings, and reasons.
