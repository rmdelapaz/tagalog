Mga Haka-haka — Hypotheticals & Complex Conditionals

The unreal and the wished-for: sana, counterfactual kung, and kung sakaling

Lesson 9 handled real conditions — if it rains, when payday comes. B2 adds the unreal: things you wish were true, things that would have happened, situations you only imagine. The engine is one small, beloved word — sana — plus counterfactual kung and the formal kung sakaling.

1. From Real to Unreal

Compare the real conditional you know with its hypothetical cousin:

  • Real (Lesson 9): Kung may pera ako, bibili ako ng bahay. — If I have money, I'll buy a house. (a live possibility)
  • Unreal (this lesson): Kung may pera ako, bibili sana ako ng bahay. — If I had money, I'd buy a house. (but I don't)

The difference is sana in the result — it flags "this is contrary to fact / only wished-for."

2. sana: Hoping & Wishing

sana ("I hope / if only / would that") does two jobs, and the aspect it sits with decides which:

sana + …MeaningExample
+ contemplated (future)hope for something aheadSana umulan bukas. — I hope it rains tomorrow.
+ completed (past)wish / regret (unreal)Sana nag-aral ako. — I wish I had studied.
  • Sana gumaling ka agad. — I hope you get well soon. (hope, future)
  • Sana nakinig ako sa iyo. — I wish I had listened to you. (regret, past)
  • Sana ganito na lang lagi. — I wish it could always be like this.
The emotional workhorse. sana carries hope, longing, and gentle regret — it's in prayers ("Sana po…"), birthday wishes ("Sana lahat ng hiling mo…"), and everyday sighs ("Sana all!" — a viral phrase meaning "wish everyone could have that!"). Master it and you unlock a very human layer of Filipino.

3. Counterfactual "If": kung + past … sana

To say "if X had happened, Y would have happened" — a past that didn't occur — put the kung clause in the completed aspect and mark the result with sana:

  • Kung nag-aral ka, pumasa ka sana. — If you had studied, you would have passed. (you didn't)
  • Kung maaga tayong umalis, hindi tayo sana nahuli. — If we'd left early, we wouldn't have been late.
  • Kung nandito ka, masaya sana ako. — If you were here, I'd be happy.

The formula: Kung + [what didn't happen], [result] + sana. The sana is what makes it clearly unreal rather than a live "if."

4. kung sakaling: "In the Event That"

The formal, more hypothetical cousin of Lesson 9's sakali is kung sakaling — "in the event that / should it come to pass." It frames a remote or careful hypothetical, common in writing and official talk:

  • Kung sakaling maantala ang flight, tatawag kami. — In the event the flight is delayed, we'll call.
  • Kung sakaling hindi ako makarating, ipagpatuloy ninyo. — Should I not be able to make it, please proceed.

5. Regrets: dapat sana / sana + past

Pair dapat (should) with sana for "should have" regrets:

FrameMeaning
Dapat sana + [verb]should have (but didn't)
Sana + [completed]I wish I had…
  • Dapat sana tumawag ako, pero nakalimutan ko. — I should have called, but I forgot.
  • Sana hindi ko sinabi 'yun. — I wish I hadn't said that.

Practice — The Unreal & Wished-For

Exercise 1: Hope or regret?

Read the aspect after sana and answer hope (future) or regret (past):

  • Sana umulan bukas. → _____ (Answer: hope)
  • Sana nag-aral ako. → _____ (Answer: regret)
  • Sana gumaling ka agad. → _____ (Answer: hope)

Exercise 2: Add sana for the wished-for meaning

Fill in the word that makes the result unreal / wished-for:

  • Kung may pera ako, bibili _____ ako ng bahay. → (Answer: sana)
  • Kung nag-aral ka, pumasa ka _____. → (Answer: sana)

Exercise 3: Build the counterfactual

Give the completed (past) form for the "if it had happened" clause:

  • Kung (aral) _____ ka, pumasa ka sana. — root aral → (Answer: nag-aral)
  • Kung maaga tayong (alis) _____, hindi sana tayo nahuli. — root alis → (Answer: umalis)

Exercise 4: "In the event that"

Fill in the formal hypothetical phrase:

  • _____ maantala ang flight, tatawag kami. (in the event that) → (Answer: kung sakaling|kung sakali)

Exercise 5: "Should have" regret

Fill in the two-word phrase for "should have":

  • _____ tumawag ako, pero nakalimutan ko. (should have) → (Answer: dapat sana)
  • Sana hindi ko _____ 'yun. (past of "sabi/sinabi" — said) → (Answer: sinabi)

Where This Takes You

Hypotheticals turn on sana: with a future verb it's hope, with a past verb it's wish/regret; and kung + past … sana builds the counterfactual "would have," while kung sakaling handles the formal "in the event that." You've now got both real (Lesson 9) and unreal conditionals. Next, we sharpen how ideas relate at the sentence level: cause, result & concession — the connectors of argument.
