Katawan, Kalusugan at Kalendaryo

The body, health, and the calendar — taking care and keeping track

Two more everyday bundles round out your A1–A2 vocabulary: the words for your body and health — so you can say what hurts and ask for help — and the calendar: the days of the week and months of the year, for making plans and marking dates.

1. Ang Katawan — The Body

TagalogEnglish
ulohead
mataeye(s)
ilongnose
bibigmouth
tengaear
kamayhand
paafoot / leg
tiyanstomach
  • Masakit ang ulo ko. (My head hurts.)
  • Dalawa ang mata, isa ang ilong. (Two eyes, one nose.)

2. Kalusugan — Health

The key pattern: masakit ang [body part] for "my ___ hurts," and may [ailment] ako for "I have a ___" (using may from Lesson 10):

TagalogEnglish
masakitpainful / it hurts
sakitpain / illness
lagnatfever
ubocough
siponcold / runny nose
gamotmedicine
doktordoctor
ospitalhospital
  • May lagnat ako. (I have a fever.)
  • May sakit ako, kailangan ko ng gamot. (I'm sick, I need medicine.)
  • Masakit ang tiyan ko. (My stomach hurts.)
  • Saan ang ospital? (Where's the hospital?)
At the pharmacy: a botika is a drugstore. "May gamot ba para sa ubo?" (Is there medicine for a cough?) is a sentence worth memorizing whole.

3. Mga Araw ng Linggo — Days of the Week

The day names come from Spanish and are used every day:

TagalogEnglish
LunesMonday
MartesTuesday
MiyerkulesWednesday
HuwebesThursday
BiyernesFriday
SabadoSaturday
LinggoSunday
  • Anong araw ngayon? — Lunes ngayon. (What day is it today? — It's Monday.)
  • Sa Sabado tayo pupunta. (We'll go on Saturday.)
Watch out: Linggo means both Sunday and week — context tells you which. "Sa isang linggo" = in one week.

4. Mga Buwan — Months of the Year

Also from Spanish, and easy to recognize:

  • Kailan ang kaarawan mo? — Sa Marso. (When's your birthday? — In March.)
  • Malamig sa Disyembre. (It's cool in December.)

Practice — Everyday Essentials

  • "I have a fever." → "May _____ ako." (Answer: lagnat)
  • "I need medicine." → "Kailangan ko ng _____." (Answer: gamot)

Why This Lesson Matters

Being able to say what hurts, ask where the pharmacy is, and name the day and month are exactly the "matters of immediate need" that define A2. With these bundles you can take care of yourself, make plans, and mark the calendar — the last vocabulary pieces of your solid A1–A2 foundation.
