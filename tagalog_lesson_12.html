Pagtanggi - The Four Ways to Say No

hindi, wala, ayaw, huwag — and exactly when to use each

English gets a lot of mileage out of one word: not. Tagalog splits that job across four words, and picking the right one is mostly about what you're negating — an action, a possession, a want, or a command. Learn the four jobs and you'll never guess again.

1. hindi — the all-purpose "not"

Use hindi for the big three: actions, descriptions, and saying what something isn't. It goes right before the word it negates.

  • Verb: Hindi ako kumain. (I didn't eat.) · Hindi siya natutulog. (He isn't sleeping.)
  • Adjective: Hindi masaya. (Not happy.) · Hindi mahal. (Not expensive.)
  • Identity: Hindi ako guro. (I'm not a teacher.) · Hindi ito kape. (This isn't coffee.)

Short answer: Hindi. = "No." It's the plain, all-round no.

2. wala — "none / there isn't"

Wala is the exact opposite of may / mayroon from Lesson 10. Use it for not having and not existing — never for a plain verb.

  • May pera ako. → Wala akong pera. (I have money → I have no money.)
  • May tao. → Walang tao. (There's someone → There's no one.)
  • "May natira pa ba?" → "Wala na." (Any left? → None left.)
Watch out: it's Wala akong pera (I have no money), not Hindi akong pera. If the English idea is "have / there is," reach for wala, not hindi.

3. ayaw — "don't want / don't like"

Ayaw is the built-in negative of gusto from Lesson 11. It takes the same NG-pronouns (ko, mo, niya) and the same pattern.

  • Gusto kong kumain. → Ayaw kong kumain. (I want to eat → I don't want to eat.)
  • Gusto niya ng kape. → Ayaw niya ng kape. (She wants coffee → She doesn't want coffee.)
  • Short answer: Ayaw ko. (I don't want to. / No, thanks.)

You can say hindi gusto, but native speakers overwhelmingly use the single word ayaw.

4. huwag — "don't!" (commands)

To tell someone not to do something, use huwag — never hindi for a command. It pairs with a pronoun + the linker, just like the pseudo-verbs.

  • Huwag! (Don't!)
  • Huwag kang umalis. (Don't leave. — "you")
  • Huwag mong gawin iyan. (Don't do that. — object-focus "mo")
  • Huwag tayong pumunta. (Let's not go.)
Softening it: add po and na lang to be gentle — Huwag na po lang. ("Please, let's just not." / "Never mind.") — handy for politely declining.

5. Negating the modals

For need, can, and should (Lesson 11), just put hindi in front — except want, which becomes ayaw:

PositiveNegative
gusto (want)ayaw (don't want)
kailangan (need)hindi kailangan (don't need)
pwede (can / allowed)hindi pwede (can't / not allowed)
dapat (should)hindi dapat (shouldn't)

Practice — Just Say No

Exercise 1: Pick the right negator

Fill the blank with hindi, wala, ayaw, or huwag:

  • "I didn't eat." → "_____ ako kumain." (Answer: hindi)
  • "I have no money." → "_____ akong pera." (Answer: wala)
  • "Don't leave." → "_____ kang umalis." (Answer: huwag)
  • "I don't want to." → "_____ ko." (Answer: ayaw)

Exercise 2: Make it negative

Give the opposite (write just the negative word that replaces the positive one):

  • "May tao." → "_____ng tao." (There's no one.) (Answer: wala)
  • "Gusto kong matulog." → "_____ kong matulog." (I don't want to sleep.) (Answer: ayaw)
  • "Masaya siya." → "_____ siyang masaya." (She's not happy.) (Answer: hindi)

Exercise 3: Negate the modal

Fill in the negative form:

  • "You can't." → "_____ pwede." (Answer: hindi)
  • "You don't need to." → "_____ kailangan." (Answer: hindi)
  • "Don't!" (a command) → "_____!" (Answer: huwag)

Why This Lesson Matters

Saying "no" clearly and correctly is one of the first things you truly need in a language — declining politely, correcting a mistake, warning someone, saying you don't have or don't want something. With hindi, wala, ayaw, and huwag sorted, you can now negate anything a beginner conversation throws at you — the last big piece of your A1–A2 grammar toolkit.
